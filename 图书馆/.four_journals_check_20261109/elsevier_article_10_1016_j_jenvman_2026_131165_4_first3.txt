Abstract 31 
Sediment dredging is widely applied to mitigate sediment P loading in 32 
hypereutrophic systems, yet its long -term effectiveness under dynamic particle 33 
deposition remains poorly constrained. In this study, a one-year field incubation in Lake 34 
Taihu combined high -resolution porewater solute profiling, benthic chemical flux 35 
measurements, microbial network analysis, sediment characterization, and phosphate 36 
oxygen isotope ( δ18OP) tracing to investigate sediment P biogeochemistry following 37 
simulated-dredging with/without settling particle inputs. Dredging suppressed 38 
porewater SRP and Fe2+ accumulation, and benthic SRP flux, thereby limiting sediment 39 
P remobilization in the absence of settling particles. However, under continuous particle 40 
deposition, sediment P cycling was rapidly re -established, particularly during warm 41 
seasons. Notably, benthic SRP efflux followed a power-law relationship with sediment 42 
oxygen uptake (F SRP=0.542SOU0.410, r2 = 0.93), indicating additional P contributions 43 
from redox-mediated feedbacks, such as Fe -P remobilization, beyond organic matter 44 
remineralization alone. Microbial and phosphate solubilizing bacteria (PSB) networks 45 
were simplified yet more stable after dredging, but particle inputs weakened this effect, 46 
reinforcing coupled biogeochemical cycling. δ18OP fingerprints further revealed that 47 
post-dredging sediment recovery was primarily fueled by redeposition of re/suspended 48 
local particles rather than nonlocal inputs. Dredging triggers an interruption of intralake 49 
P recycling but should be applied alongside complementary geoengineering to optimize 50 
eutrophication management. 51
