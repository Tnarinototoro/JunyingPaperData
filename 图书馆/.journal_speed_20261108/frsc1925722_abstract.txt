Abstract
Public green spaces are essential for urban well-being, social inclusion, and environmental sustainability. However, access to these spaces is often unevenly distributed, particularly in large, consolidated cities. This study examines environmental justice in access to public green spaces in the Guadalajara Metropolitan Area, México, one of the largest urban regions in Latin America. Using a mixed methods approach, we combined Geographic Information Systems (GIS), socioeconomic indicators and social perceptions surveys to evaluate the distribution, accessibility, and use of public green space across different levels of socioeconomic marginalization. Accessibility was measured using pedestrian network distances of 300 m and 500 m. Results showed strong spatial inequalities. Only 23% of the population lives within 300 m of a public green space and 49% within 500 m. Areas with very low and low marginalization account for 55% of the metropolitan population but represent nearly 70% of residents with access within 300 m. In contrast, populations living in highly marginalized areas remain strongly underrepresented even when the accessibility threshold increases to 500 m. Green space availability per capita also differed significantly among municipalities and marginalization levels, with the lowest values concentrated in highly marginalized neighborhoods. Survey results, based on a self-selected online sample predominantly representing low-to medium-marginalization residents, evidenced that proximity alone doesn't guarantee park use. Travel time was negatively associated with visitation frequency, while insecurity, small park size, and poor infrastructure were identified as major barriers. These findings showed that public green spaces function not only as environmental infrastructure but also as indicators of urban equity. The study highlights the need to incorporate environmental justice criteria into urban planning and green infrastructure policies to reduce socio-spatial inequalities and support more inclusive and sustainable cities.
