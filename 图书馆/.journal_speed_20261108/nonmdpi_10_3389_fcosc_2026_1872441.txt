You can register here Notify me Abstract 1. To effectively conserve biodiversity within protected areas (PA), the inclusion, cooperation, and support of local communities is essential. Among the most important predictors of local support are PA-derived benefits, but despite their importance, the directionality and strength of benefit flows from PAs to local communities remains under-quantified. 2. Here, we employed a perceptions-based approach to document and characterise the benefit flows that members of local communities receive from living near Murchison Falls National Park (MFNP), Uganda. We then sought to assess to what extent these perceived benefits impact support for conservation. We collected perceptions via structured household surveys in villages surrounding the periphery of MFNP. 3. Survey responses highlighted that, although the majority of people perceived benefits from living near MFNP, supporting ecosystem services and intangible benefits were the most common, and benefits were not distributed equally around the park. 4. Household relationship with rangers was the biggest predictor of level of conservation support. Alongside the respondents' gender, perceived positive impact from MFNP, and role in community decision-making. 5. Our results elucidate current perceived MFNP benefit flows and can be used to evidence and evaluate future conservation projects, and ultimately contribute to the more effective and inclusive management of MFNP.
