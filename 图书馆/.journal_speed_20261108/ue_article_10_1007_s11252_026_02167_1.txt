Keke 1 Show authors 9 Accesses 1 Altmetric Explore all metrics Abstract Urban expansion is changing bird assemblages across sub-Saharan Africa, where the sorting of species along urban–rural gradients is understudied. We present a multi-scale analysis of bird assemblages at four locations in Minna, central Nigeria, combining trait-environment modelling, ordination, beta-diversity analysis and machine learning. We recorded 115 species (15 orders, 50 families) in 71 single-visit, 10-minute, fixed-radius point counts (April to July 2021). Because locations were sampled at different times, location and season are partly confounded. Bird detection was weakly predicted (AUC = 0.676): generalists and frugivorous birds were more likely to be detected at multiple stations compared to specialists. Abundance where detected was weakly predicted (R² = 0.133) and higher among seed-eating species. Trait-environment interactions did not improve prediction, indicating trait-associated detection rather than trait-mediated environmental filtering. No community metric was predictable at a withheld location, and no feeding-guild model had predictive skill. Composition differed among locations (PERMANOVA pseudo-F = 1.68, R² = 0.070, p = 0.001), although dispersion also differed and the three locations surveyed within nine days did not differ significantly. Beta-diversity was mostly explained by replacement (0.65) compared to nestedness (0.07). Shannon diversity and functional dispersion were lowest in Gidan Kwano, whereas richness did not differ, even after rarefaction. Composition was associated with tree density, tree size, and elevation, yet not independent from geographic distance. Turnover-driven replacement suggests that networks of habitat patches would provide better protection of regional diversity, which should be confirmed through repeated surveys at same seasons. Ethics declarations Ethics approval This study was based entirely on observational point-count surveys of free-living wild birds detected by sight and sound. No animals were captured, handled, marked, or otherwise manipulated, and no specimens were collected. As the surveys were purely observational and non-invasive, no ethics approval or collection permit was required. Consent to participate Not applicable; the study did not involve human participants.
