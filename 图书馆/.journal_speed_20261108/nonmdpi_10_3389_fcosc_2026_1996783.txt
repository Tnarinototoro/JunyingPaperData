You can register here Notify me Abstract Elephant poaching and the ivory trade remain major conservation challenges, making effective public communication an important component of demand reduction and policy support. This study examines how international conservation NGOs frame elephant-ivory conservation across organizational and national contexts and how such communication may reflect the localization of global conservation norms. Drawing on framing theory and norm localization, it analyzes 32 WWF and IFAW campaign documents from the United States and China between 2015 and 2025 using mixed deductive-inductive content analysis. Results reveal patterned but nonuniform variation: China-based materials emphasize progress-oriented framing, while U.S.-based materials show stronger scientific emphasis in different communication dimensions. Organizational patterns also differ across contexts. Rather than indicating a uniform U.S.-China or WWF-IFAW divide, the findings show that shared conservation objectives are articulated through different combinations of problems, solutions, values, evidence, and tone, linking conservation communication with norm-localization scholarship.
