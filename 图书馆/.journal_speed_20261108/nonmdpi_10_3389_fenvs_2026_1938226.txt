School of Economics and Management, North China Electric Power University, Beijing, China See more Article metrics View details Abstract In the context of China’s dual carbon goals, the electricity sector is both the primary source of carbon emissions and a key driver of socioeconomic development. A systematic assessment of the evolution of electricity carbon emission factors is critical for tracking electricity system decarbonization and formulating effective emission-reduction policies. This study develops an integrated analytical framework for examining the spatiotemporal heterogeneity and international positioning of electricity carbon emission factors of China. Based on official national- and provincial-level data, the framework combines the statistical analysis modules including spatiotemporal characteristics analysis, spatial autocorrelation analysis, policy-stage comparison, and international benchmarking to characterize the ECEF patterns. Results show that China’s electricity sector has achieved substantial low-carbon transition progress. Based on the available benchmark-year observations, China’s national average ECEF decreased by 13.03% between 2015 and 2023, while interprovincial disparities widened and maintained a “high in the north, low in the south” spatial pattern. The spatial correlation structure has continuously evolved, with weakened global positive spatial autocorrelation, contracted High-High and Low-Low clusters, and strengthened High-Low heterogeneity, indicating diminished geographic proximity effects and more complex spatial differentiation. International comparisons showed that China’s 2024 ECEF was above the mean values of the EU and Umbrella samples and slightly below the median of the developing-country samples, suggesting considerable transition pressure and emission-reduction potential. These findings offer decision support for differentiated regional low-carbon transitions of the electricity sector. Graphical Abstract 1 Introduction Global carbon dioxide emissions have become a primary driver of the climate crisis, accelerating the low-carbon transition of the world’s energy system (International Energy Agency, 2023; Zscheischler and Fischer, 2020). In China, the electricity sector remains the largest single source of carbon emissions, responsible for approximately 42% of national emissions in 2021 (National Development and Reform Commission, 2021). This stems primarily from China’s fossil fuel-dominated power generation mix, which creates a strong positive correlation between electricity consumption and CO2 emissions (Liu, 2023). At the same time, electrification has become a major pillar of high-quality economic development and a cornerstone of China’s dual carbon goals. The decarbonization of the electricity sector will therefore directly determine the scale of national emission reductions and the feasibility of achieving carbon peaking and carbon neutrality (Deng et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2024). For this reason, systematic research on the low-carbon transition of the electricity sector is highly relevant and practically important. The electricity carbon emission factor (ECEF) serves as a core metric for quantifying the carbon intensity of power supply, representing the volume of CO2 emitted per unit of electricity consumed (Tollefson, 2022). Under the methodological framework developed by the IPCC, this factor has been widely adopted worldwide as a practical and reliable tool for carbon measurement and evaluation (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, 2006). It not only reflects the cleanliness of the power system, but also supports the assessment of emission reductions from electrification, ensures fairness in carbon market transactions, and underpins the credibility of green electricity certification (European Commission, 2022). A comprehensive understanding of ECEF is therefore essential for evaluating the decarbonization trajectory of the power system. Numerous studies have explored the calculation and application of ECEF. Methodologically, the IPCC emission factor approach has become the global standard. In practice, the factor is widely used in life-cycle assessment, sectoral emission quantification (Mastrucci et al., 2024; He and Yang, 2024), and the development of provincial and municipal carbon inventories, particularly for measuring Scope 2 indirect emissions (Wu et al., 2025). Despite these advances, most existing studies treat the ECEF as a static parameter, leaving its dynamic evolution and spatiotemporal variations largely understudied. Against this backdrop, two critical research gaps exist in the literature. First, few studies investigate the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of provincial ECEF or quantify the regional ECEF change before and after the announcement of the dual-carbon goals using official provincial data. Most research relies on IPCC default values or third-party datasets such as CEADs and MEIC, which may deviate from official statistical definitions (Shan et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2025; Han et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2025). Moreover, existing analyses of the dual carbon goals rely heavily on qualitative interpretation or long-term scenario projections (Zhang et al., 2024), lacking rigorous quantitative evidence to identify how these policies have shaped the evolution of ECEF. Second, micro-level international positioning based on ECEF remains limited. Current cross-country comparisons focus on macro-level indicators such as national carbon intensity, per capita emissions, and decoupling rates. Studies that benchmark China against major global climate negotiation blocs using the ECEF are rare, making it difficult to objectively assess China’s global standing and decarbonization potential. To address these gaps, this study uses official provincial ECEF data to examine spatiotemporal heterogeneity and quantify the policy-stage comparison of the dual carbon goals. We further compare China with key climate negotiation blocs, including the European Union (EU), the Umbrella Group, and the Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS), to establish the international positioning of China’s ECEF. The findings aim to support differentiated low-carbon transition strategies for regional power systems and strengthen China’s agenda-setting role in global climate governance. The contribution of this study lies in integrating temporal, spatial, policy-stage, and international perspectives within a unified empirical framework. Specifically, national-level benchmark observations are used to characterize temporal changes in ECEF, provincial data are used to examine spatial heterogeneity and spatial association, policy-stage comparisons are used to assess changes before and after the introduction of China’s dual-carbon goals, and cross-country data are used to position China within the international distribution of electricity carbon emission factors. By integrating these complementary dimensions, this study provides a systematic empirical characterization framework of ECEF heterogeneity and its evolution, rather than proposing new statistical methods. 2 Methodology and data 2.1 Analytical framework This study develops an integrated analytical framework for examining the spatiotemporal heterogeneity and international positioning of ECEF, as shown in Figure 1. Rather than introducing new statistical methods, the framework integrates established analytical approaches to examine ECEF from the perspective of spatio-temporal characteristics, spatial correlations, policy-stage comparison and international comparison. The framework consists of three core modules. FIGURE 1 Analytical framework of this study. First, the spatiotemporal evolution module. Based on provincial power grid carbon emission factor data released by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment of China (Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China, 2018a; Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China and National Bureau of Statistics, 2024; National Development and Reform Commission, 2018a; National Development and Reform Commission, 2018b), descriptive statistics are first used to characterize the temporal evolution trend of national ECEF and the spatial distribution patterns across seven major geographic regions. Third, the international comparison and positioning module. Box plots are used for visual comparison, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) is applied to test the statistical significance of differences in ECEF among different country groups, so as to clarify the relative international position of China’s ECEF. Based on the available national benchmark-year observations, China’s national ECEF decreased from 0.6101 kg CO2/kWh in 2015 to 0.5306 kg CO2/kWh in 2023, corresponding to an overall decline of 13.03%. The available intermediate observations for 2021 and 2022 are also lower than the 2015 level. Because national-level observations are available only for selected benchmark years, this result represents the overall change between the observed benchmark years rather than evidence of a continuous year-by-year decline (National Development and Reform Commission, 2017). From a spatial perspective, the seven major regions in China exhibit pronounced heterogeneity in ECEF. The medians shown in the box plots differ substantially across regions, with North China and Northwest China displaying significantly higher values than South China and Southwest China. This pattern suggests a systematic regional divergence in energy structures, where regions with higher emission factors are likely characterized by a greater reliance on fossil fuel-based power generation, while regions with lower values benefit from a higher share of low-carbon energy sources such as hydropower and nuclear power. This finding is consistent with previous studies that highlight significant regional disparities in carbon intensity across China (Zhou and Han, 2016). Furthermore, the box lengths and violin plot distributions indicate varying degrees of intra-regional dispersion. Regions such as North China, East China, Central China, and Northeast China exhibit relatively compact distributions, suggesting lower variability among provinces. In contrast, Northwest and Southwest China display more dispersed distributions, reflecting greater heterogeneity within regions. These differences may be associated with variations in regional energy endowments, grid integration levels, and industrial structures, which jointly influence the distribution of ECEF. This indicates that the spatial dependence of provincial ECEF strengthened during the earlier stage of the study period but subsequently weakened, demonstrating a dynamic rather than continuously declining pattern of spatial association. 3.2.2 Local Moran’s index analysis Local Moran’s I is employed to further identify the specific spatial clustering patterns of ECEF across provinces. Based on the LISA cluster maps, provinces can be classified into four categories: High-High (HH), Low-Low (LL), High-Low (HL), and Low-High (LH), representing different types of local spatial association. This classification provides a more disaggregated perspective on spatial dependence, enabling the identification of localized clusters and spatial outliers in the distribution of ECEFs. When analyzing local spatial autocorrelation patterns, priority should be given to selecting the weight matrix that best reflects overall spatial dependence. By comparing the global Moran’s I values across three weight matrices, this study found that the adjacency matrix yielded consistently higher Moran’s I values than the other two matrices. This indicates that spatial variation in provincial ECEF is more strongly associated with geographic adjacency than with purely economic or composite distances. As a result, Table 3 summarizes the changes in exploratory local spatial patterns between 2010 and 2023. The number of candidate High–High (HH) patterns decreased from six in 2010 to four in 2023. Beijing and Shandong transitioned out of the exploratory HH category, while Shanxi, Hebei, Inner Mongolia, and Tianjin retained this classification. The number of candidate Low–Low (LL) patterns decreased from twelve to eight, with Fujian, Hubei, Chongqing, and Guizhou transitioning out of the exploratory LL category. Sichuan, Yunnan, and Qinghai, all characterized by relatively low-carbon electricity structures, retained the exploratory LL classification in both periods. Specifically, the three country groups included in the inferential analysis comprised 24 EU countries, 11 Umbrella Group countries, and 12 developing countries. The mean ECEF values were 0.2265 kg CO2/kWh for the EU group, 0.2994 kg CO2/kWh for the Umbrella Group, and 0.5497 kg CO2/kWh for the developing-country group. Shapiro–Wilk tests did not indicate significant departures from normality in any of the three groups, and Levene’s test did not reject the assumption of homogeneity of variances (p = 0.063).One-way ANOVA indicated a significant overall difference among the three groups (Table 6, F (2,44) = 12.168, p < 0.001). This result was also supported by Welch’s ANOVA (p < 0.001) and the Kruskal–Wallis test (p < 0.001). Furthermore, Tukey HSD post hoc comparisons showed that the developing-country group had a significantly higher mean ECEF than the EU group (mean difference = 0.3232 kg CO2/kWh, 95% CI: 0.1633–0.4831, p < 0.001) and the Umbrella Group (mean difference = 0.2503 kg CO2/kWh, 95% CI: 0.0615–0.4391, p = 0.0068), whereas the difference between the EU and Umbrella groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.535). To assess the influence of differences in observation year and data source, a sensitivity analysis was conducted using only Ember observations for 2024, yielding a harmonized subsample of 37 countries (EU: n = 20; Umbrella Group: n = 9; developing countries: n = 8). The overall group difference remained significant (one-way ANOVA: F (2,34) = 6.736, p = 0.003; Welch ANOVA: p = 0.011; Kruskal–Wallis: p = 0.011). In this harmonized subsample, the difference between the developing-country and EU groups remained statistically significant, whereas the developing-country–Umbrella and EU–Umbrella comparisons were not statistically significant. These results indicate that the higher ECEF of the developing-country group relative to the EU group is comparatively robust to harmonization of observation year and data source, whereas the difference between the developing-country and Umbrella groups is more sensitive to sample composition. 4 Discussion From 2015 to 2023, the national average ECEFs exhibited a declining trend, while interprovincial disparities widened. These empirical findings indicate that while China has made notable progress in the electricity sector toward its dual carbon goals at the national level, regional inequalities have intensified. Moreover, the results show the evolving spatiotemporal distribution patterns of emissions and position China within the broader global energy transition. 4.1 Temporal analysis At the national level, the decline in China’s average ECEF stems from the combined effects of national energy strategy and technological advancement, primarily driven by energy mix optimization and improved energy efficiency. Driven by national policies, renewable energy generation, particularly wind and solar power, has expanded rapidly (Su, 2024). The share of non-fossil energy in total electricity generation has increased annually, structurally diluting the proportion of high-carbon coal-fired power and becoming the dominant force in reducing the national grid’s average emission factor. Concurrently, the thermal power sector has undergone technological iteration, progressively replacing inefficient small-scale units with high-parameter, large-capacity coal-fired units. This transition has contributed to fossil fuel consumption per unit of electricity generated (Zhang et al., 2025), providing critical support for the overall decline in carbon emission intensity. At the interprovincial level, the standard deviation and range of interprovincial ECEFs showed an expanding trend throughout the study period, becoming particularly pronounced in the latter stages. This divergence primarily stems from regional specialization under the national strategy for optimizing energy resource allocation. Coal-rich provinces like Shanxi, Inner Mongolia, and Xinjiang, serving as national power export bases, have long relied on coal-fired power generation. Consequently, their decarbonization progress has been relatively slow, with emission factors remaining persistently high. In contrast, electricity consumption hubs in eastern coastal regions have significantly optimized their electricity mix through large-scale imports of clean power (such as hydropower and wind power) from central and western China via initiatives like the West-East Power Transmission Project. This has resulted in emission factors at a relatively low level. Furthermore, major hydropower provinces like Sichuan and Yunnan inherently maintain extremely low emission factors due to their clean energy mix. Traditional industrial provinces such as Liaoning have achieved significant reductions in energy and carbon intensity by developing high-tech manufacturing and optimizing industrial structures, offering valuable lessons for similar regions undergoing transformation (Lin, 2025). Thus, the widening interprovincial disparities represent a distributional effect arising from the national pursuit of overall energy allocation efficiency. While cross-regional power transmission networks optimize the overall energy landscape, they also objectively lead to divergences among regions in decarbonization progress and emission levels. 4.2 Transformation of spatial dependence The spatio-temporal autocorrelation analysis reveals that the spatial distribution characteristics of China’s ECEFs are evolving profoundly. The decline in the global Moran’s I indicates a weakening positive spatial autocorrelation among provincial emission factors, suggesting that a province’s emission factor is becoming less influenced by its geographic neighbors. This shift in spatial association patterns may be attributed to the expansion of cross-regional power transmission networks, particularly ultra-high voltage (UHV) technology. Traditionally, regional power structures were primarily determined by local resource endowments and electricity exchanges with neighboring areas, constrained by transmission losses and technological limitations. However, UHV grids have enabled large-scale, long-distance power transmission, thereby transcending geographical barriers (Wang et al., 2023). 4.3 International comparison and policy implications As China represents a single country observation within the developing-country group, the statistical differences identified by the abovementioned ANOVA and subsequent post hoc tests cannot be interpreted as direct significance tests between China and the EU, Umbrella, or developing-country groups. China’s position is therefore assessed descriptively. Specifically, China’s ECEF was 0.5596 kg CO2/kWh, which was above the mean values of the EU (0.2265 kg CO2/kWh) and Umbrella (0.2994 kg CO2/kWh) groups and close to the mean of the developing-country group (0.5497 kg CO2/kWh). Within the developing-country samples, China’s ECEF was slightly below the group median (0.5882 kg CO2/kWh). This international comparison offers an objective quantitative benchmark for understanding China’s electricity-sector decarbonization status in a global context. The observed gap in the ECEF reflects inherent stage-related differences in economic development and electricity -generation mixes across nations. Although constrained by its development stage, China has achieved a comparatively favorable ECEF level among developing-country economies. Therefore, based on the aforementioned dynamic evolution characteristics, future emission reduction policies should be more refined and adaptive. For successful cases such as Liaoning, which achieved emission reductions by optimizing the industrial structure and developing high-tech industries, their transformation experience should be summarized. These regions should be encouraged to transfer technology, capital, and management expertise to neighboring provinces still located in the HH cluster. For most non-significant regions, policy formulation should be based on independent assessments of each province’s energy endowment, industrial structure, and technological level to identify emission reduction potential and constraints, and to design customized incentive and restraint mechanisms. 5 Conclusion This paper systematically analyzed the spatiotemporal evolution of China’s ECEFs and the international positioning of China in the context of the net-zero by 2050. Based on empirical analysis and theoretical review, the main conclusions are as follows. First, China’s electricity sector has achieved remarkable results in the low-carbon transition, while temporal and spatial disparities have become increasingly prominent. From a temporal perspective, based on the available benchmark-year observations, the national average ECEF decreased by 13.03% between 2015 and 2023. From a spatial perspective, provincial ECEFs show significant spatial heterogeneity and a stable pattern, high in the north while low in the south. Comparisons before and after the announcement of the dual-carbon goals indicate regional reductions in ECEF alongside widening interprovincial disparities. Second, the spatial autocorrelation patterns of the ECEFs are undergoing a transformation, with the traditional influence of geographic proximity diminishing. The positive spatial autocorrelation of provincial ECEF showed a declining trend in the later stage, indicating a weakening of spatial dependence between provinces. Both HH-type and LL-type candidate patterns may have contracted toward core resource-endowed regions between 2010 and 2023, while HL-type candidate outliers may have expanded. This reflects increasing complexity and dynamism in China’s national spatial patterns. Third, internationally, significant differences in terms of ECEF were observed among the EU, Umbrella, and developing-country groups. The developing-country group exhibited a higher mean value of ECEF than that of the EU group, a result that remained supported by the sensitivity analysis. Furthermore, China’s ECEF was above the mean values of the EU and Umbrella groups and slightly below the median of the developing-country sample, indicating that further decarbonization of the electricity system remains very important.
