Nelson 2 , Mary Powell 3 & … Josh Gilbert 4 Show authors 13 Accesses Explore all metrics Abstract Urban riverscapes contribute substantially to the ecological function, livability, and sustainability of urban and suburban environments, yet their conservation and restoration require assessment approaches that capture interacting ecological, hydrologic, geomorphic, and social dimensions across spatial scales. This article presents URBAN—the Urban Riverscape conditions-Based Assessment for management Needs—as a flexible method for implementing the Technical Domain of the Urban Riverscapes Assessment Framework. URBAN is distinctive in explicitly linking Human Connections and Values with physical processes, community priorities, and anthropogenic stressors that shape long-term riverscape recovery. The assessment is organized around four Facets: Human Connections and Values, Hydrologic Processes and Hydraulic Characteristics, Geomorphic Forms and Processes, and Ecological Structure and Processes. Applied in metropolitan Denver, Colorado, USA, URBAN integrates geospatial analysis, rapid field observations, reach typing, response-potential analysis, and scoring criteria to identify stressors, functional conditions, and management-relevant outcomes. Results from the Denver application indicate that functional conditions and community values may align, diverge, or conflict across reaches, underscoring the need for synthesis across Metrics, Indicators, and Facets rather than reliance on isolated measures. In combination with GIS tools and the broader Urban Riverscape Framework, URBAN provides an objective, multi-scale approach for interpreting multi-causal mechanisms, prioritizing preservation and restoration actions, and supporting planning, regulatory, and engagement needs. The Denver case study demonstrates the utility of URBAN for watershed-scale planning, reach-scale prioritization, and transparent communication among managers, practitioners, and communities, while maintaining adaptability to local context and data availability.
