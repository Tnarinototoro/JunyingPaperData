You can register here Notify me Abstract Green innovation can support corporate decarbonization, but its relationship with firms' carbon performance requires empirical examination. This study investigates the relationship between green innovation and carbon intensity among Chinese A-share listed companies using data from the China Stock Market and Accounting Research (CSMAR) database for 2010-2023. We estimate models with firm and year fixed effects and employ lagged explanatory variables, instrumental variable estimation, and two-step system Generalized Method of Moments (GMM) to address potential endogeneity and dynamic persistence. The results reveal a negative and statistically significant association between green innovation and carbon intensity. In the fully controlled baseline specification, a 10% increase in green patent applications is associated with approximately a 0.1% decrease in carbon intensity. The negative relationship persists across the additional estimation approaches and remains robust to alternative patent transformations, carbon-intensity reporting precision, financial-ratio winsorization, and various sample observations. Subsample analyses demonstrate large heterogeneity in this relationship. These findings provide firm-level evidence linking green inventive activity to lower emissions per unit of revenue and identify the corporate settings in which this relationship is most clearly observed. The study informs the assessment of green innovation as a potential contributor to improved corporate carbon performance.
