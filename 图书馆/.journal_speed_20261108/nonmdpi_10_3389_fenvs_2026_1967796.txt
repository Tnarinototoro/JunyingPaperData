You can register here Notify me Abstract Environmental taxation, energy consumption, and tourism are closely related to economic performance in Mediterranean economies. This study examines these relationships in seven EU Mediterranean countries (Greece, Spain, Croatia, Italy, Cyprus, France, and Slovenia) from 1995 to 2023. It uses the Cross-Sectionally Augmented ARDL (CS-ARDL) model, which accounts for cross-sectional dependence and slope heterogeneity, and a Panel VAR model to examine how environmental taxation, energy consumption, and tourism activity are related to GDP per capita. The analysis is complemented by extensive diagnostic and robustness checks: collinearity and serial correlation tests, structural-break dummies, panel cointegration tests, a Panel VECM, an alternative tax measure, greenhouse gas emissions as an alternative outcome, and leave-one-country-out estimates. In our preferred CS-ARDL specification, which includes capital, labour and break dummies, gross fixed capital formation per inhabitant is positively and significantly associated with GDP per capita and tourism intensity is positive but only marginally significant while energy consumption and the implicit energy tax rate are not significant. Energy consumption is positively associated with GDP per capita only in the models without capital and labour. In the Panel VAR, tourism Granger-causes GDP per capita growth, while energy consumption and environmental taxation do not. Cointegration tests give mixed evidence, and adding an error-correction term to the Panel VAR does not change these results.
