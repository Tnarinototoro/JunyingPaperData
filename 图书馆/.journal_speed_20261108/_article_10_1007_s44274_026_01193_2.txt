Jaiswal 3 Show authors 156 Accesses Explore all metrics Abstract Sustainable waste management and resource recovery are increasingly important components of circular economy and sustainability transitions, yet the intellectual structure and thematic evolution of this interconnected research domain remain insufficiently characterized. Previous bibliometric studies have largely examined individual dimensions of waste management, recycling, circular economy, or resource recovery, leaving their broader interrelationships comparatively fragmented. This study provides a global bibliometric assessment of sustainable waste management and resource recovery research published between 2015 and 2025, with particular emphasis on the evolution of research activity, intellectual and collaborative structures, and thematic priorities. A total of 19,473 English-language research articles indexed in Scopus were analyzed using bibliometric indicators and VOSviewer-based network visualization. The analysis examined publication and citation patterns, leading journals, authors, institutions, countries, funding agencies, collaboration networks, intellectual structures, and keyword co-occurrence patterns. The results show a substantial increase in scientific output during the study period, indicating growing scholarly attention to sustainable waste management, resource recovery, circular economy, climate change, and environmental sustainability. China, India, the United States, and the United Kingdom emerged as major contributors, with extensive international collaboration characterizing the research landscape. Keyword analysis revealed four interconnected thematic pillars: sustainable waste management and circular economy; wastewater treatment and pollution control; resource recovery and bioenergy generation; and environmental impact assessment and climate change mitigation. The thematic structure indicates an increasing integration of waste management with resource efficiency, waste valorization, environmental assessment, and circular economy concepts, while digital technologies such as artificial intelligence, Internet of Things, and blockchain are emerging research areas. Overall, the findings indicate an evolution in research themes, publication patterns, and knowledge structures from predominantly conventional waste-management concerns toward more integrated resource-recovery, circular-economy, and sustainability-oriented research. The study provides an integrated knowledge -domain perspective that can support researchers, policymakers, funding agencies, and industry stakeholders in identifying research priorities, collaboration opportunities, and emerging areas for investigation. Importantly, the observed bibliometric trends represent changes in scholarly activity and thematic development and should not be interpreted as direct evidence of technological effectiveness, environmental performance, economic feasibility, or policy implementation success. Cultural Resource Management Environmental Management Environmental Archaeology Resource and Environmental Economics Waste Management/Waste Technology Corporate Environmental Management Circular Economy Strategies for Sustainable Development 1 Introduction Urbanization, industrialization, economic growth, and changes in consumption patterns have resulted in an increase in waste production worldwide, posing major environmental, economic, and social problems [ 1 , 2 ]. According to the World Bank, global municipal solid waste production is expected to keep growing throughout the decades to come [ 3 ]. Poor waste collection, improper waste disposal, and low waste recovery rate lead to various forms of environmental degradation including pollution of water sources and atmosphere, greenhouse gas emission, and loss of biodiversity, among others [ 4 ]. Waste management has thus become one of the most important sustainable development issues facing the planet today. It is not only concerned with the environment but also resource security and economic development [ 5 ]. To overcome such difficulties, the idea of sustainable waste management has been steadily emerging as an integral part of global environmental and developmental discourse. The goal of sustainable waste management is aimed at the minimization of the production of waste, increased use and recycling of resources, and decreasing negative effects related to the implementation of traditional ways of waste handling [ 6 ]. All the mentioned targets correlate with the goals set out in the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals framework, including Goal 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities), Goal 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), Goal 13 (Climate Action), and Goal 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation). In addition, the transition to circular economy models has further cemented the perception of waste as an important source of productivity. The circular economy paradigm has become a revolutionary strategy for breaking the link between economic growth and resource use by emphasizing resource efficiency, recycling, waste valorization, and sustainable manufacturing processes [ 7 , 8 ]. While the conventional linear system involves “take-make-dispose,” circular economy approaches focus on closed-loop material circulation to prolong the lifecycle of resources and reduce waste creation [ 9 , 10 ]. In this regard, sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems constitute an important component of the circular economy by engaging in recycling, composting, anaerobic digestion, waste to energy conversion, wastewater treatment, and material recovery initiatives [ 11 , 12 ]. In the last ten years, significant strides have been made in scientific research concerning waste management and resource recovery. Areas of study include environmental sciences, engineering, energy, urban planning, economics, public policies, and sustainability sciences. Scientific research is becoming important for the realization of waste management issues that call for integrative solutions, which involve an environmental, technological, institutional, and socioeconomic perspective to solving waste problems [ 13 , 14 ]. Research areas have included recycling processes, pathways to resource recovery, assessment of environmental impacts, climate change mitigation, implementation of the circular economy, and sustainable urban development among others. Digital technology solutions, including the use of artificial intelligence, Internet of things (IoT), and data collection tools have also gained traction, but they remain emerging fields in the sustainable waste management literature [ 15 , 16 , 17 ]. Although there have been remarkable advancements made in science, several gaps still exist when it comes to waste management capabilities, technological advancements, and environmental governance between developing and developed parts of the world. Developing nations experience a surge in waste production due to increased urbanization and rapid population growth, but at the same time, they lack adequate funding, inefficient waste collection, poor regulatory measures, and proper waste treatment technologies [ 18 , 19 ]. This scenario creates numerous environmental hazards due to open dump sites and improper waste handling techniques in most developing regions. As far as environmental development is concerned, sustainable waste management is more than a technological challenge; it involves social equity as well as the principles of environmental justice. Environmental risks related to waste are more likely to affect marginalized communities who lack access to environmental goods and decision-making mechanisms [ 20 , 21 ]. Furthermore, there are many informal waste workers in developing nations who make great contributions to recycling and resource recovery; nevertheless, they suffer from poor working conditions. Therefore, consideration of the social aspects of the problem is necessary for making the transition towards sustainability [ 22 , 23 , 24 ]. Considering the fast-growing and ever-increasing complexity of this discipline, there is a need to systematically assess the intellectual structure, evolution of research, and emerging thematic foci of the field. Bibliometrics offers a robust tool for exploring various aspects of a particular domain of research such as publishing patterns, networks of collaboration, leading researchers, and structural knowledge bases, among others [ 25 , 26 ]. Such an assessment can offer important insights to scientists, policymakers, donors, and development practitioners. Previous bibliometric investigations have examined specific dimensions of waste management, recycling, circular economy, construction and demolition waste, green technologies, and sustainable waste-management practices. However, the literature remains fragmented across these thematic domains. A comprehensive knowledge-domain assessment integrating sustainable waste management, resource recovery, circular economy, and sustainability transitions within a single analytical framework remains comparatively limited [ 27 , 28 , 29 ]. Therefore, the present study maps the intellectual, thematic, collaborative, institutional, and funding structures of this interconnected research domain during 2015–2025. Rather than interpreting publication growth as evidence of real-world environmental or technological progress, this study examines how research priorities, knowledge structures, collaboration patterns, and thematic relationships have evolved over time. Accordingly, this study aims to provide a global bibliometric assessment of sustainable waste management and resource recovery research from 2015 to 2025. The specific objectives are to (i) analyze publication productivity and performance; (ii) identify key journals, authors, institutions, countries, and funding agencies; (iii) examine collaboration patterns and intellectual structures; (iv) identify dominant, emerging, and evolving research themes; and (v) examine how sustainable waste management and resource recovery research is connected to themes associated with circular economy and broader sustainability objectives. This study provides insights into research themes relevant to sustainable development, resource efficiency, environmental resilience, and sustainability transitions. 2 Methodology 2.1 Data source and search strategy Figure 1 shows the detailed workflow adopted in literature retrieval and screening for the identification of articles in sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems. The literature search was undertaken on 27 April 2026 in the Scopus database using a range of keyword searches related to waste management, technological developments, sustainability, circular economy, and resource recovery systems for 2015 to 2025. In the first search, a total of 34,735 articles were generated. In the next stage, 14,729 non-research documents were excluded consisting of reviews, books, book chapters, editorials, conference proceedings, letters, notes, and errata, leaving a total of 20,006 studies. Next, 533 non-English language articles were excluded to yield a final set of 19,473 studies for analysis. Scopus was selected as the primary bibliographic database because of its broad multidisciplinary coverage across environmental sciences, engineering, energy, sustainability, economics, and social sciences, which collectively represent the interdisciplinary nature of sustainable waste management and resource recovery research. Scopus also provides structured bibliographic metadata, citation information, author affiliations, country information, funding information, and keywords that are suitable for reproducible bibliometric and network analyses using VOSviewer. The use of a single database further ensured consistency in metadata structure and avoided complications associated with differences in indexing, citation coverage, and metadata harmonization among multiple databases. Nevertheless, reliance on Scopus alone may introduce database-specific coverage bias. Consequently, the findings represent the Scopus-indexed research landscape rather than the complete global scholarly literature on sustainable waste management and resource recovery. Search query (TITLE-ABS-KEY ( ( “smart waste management” OR “waste management” OR “waste recycling” OR “waste reduction” OR “waste treatment” OR “waste disposal” ) AND ( “technology” OR “technologies” OR “innovation” OR “system” OR “solution” ) AND ( “sustainable” OR “sustainability” OR “resource recovery” OR “circular economy” OR “environmental impact” OR “resource management” ) ) AND PUBYEAR > 2014 AND PUBYEAR < 2026 AND ( LIMIT-TO ( DOCTYPE,“ar” ) ) AND ( LIMIT-TO ( LANGUAGE,“English” ) ) ). In addition, articles including review articles, book chapters, book, note, editorial, retracted, conference review, short survey, and erratum were excluded from the study and only those articles which have been published in the English language were included. Only research articles were retained for the final analysis to maintain consistency in publication type, citation patterns, bibliographic metadata, and analytical comparability. Conference proceedings, book chapters, books, editorials, notes, reviews, and other document types were excluded because their publication and citation characteristics differ substantially from those of research articles. Nevertheless, this restriction may exclude emerging research, particularly early-stage technological developments and conceptual contributions that initially appear in conference proceedings or book chapters. This limitation should therefore be considered when interpreting the comprehensiveness of the dataset. 2.3 Bibliometric indicators Some of the bibliometric indicators taken into consideration for the study include annual publications, citation analysis, co-authorship analysis, keyword co-occurrence analysis, and co-citation analysis. Bibliometric indicators were used to identify relationships among researchers, publications, and research themes [ 30 , 31 ]. For example, Co-authorship analysis identifies collaboration patterns among researchers to make useful associations that lead to transformation; Keyword co-occurrence analysis identifies prominent and emerging research themes, while the co-citation analysis indicator enables one to identify the building blocks of literature as well as intellectual concepts. This strategy allows each visualization to provide a response to an important issue in the study area. Recent bibliometric studies in sustainability-oriented domains have demonstrated the value of combining conventional performance indicators with network-based visualization and thematic knowledge-domain mapping. Moustafa [ 30 , 31 ], for example, used bibliometric and visualization approaches to characterize the evolution of sustainability-related research themes, highlighting the value of integrating publication trends with thematic and knowledge-structure analyses. Building on these approaches, the present study combines performance indicators with co-authorship, co-citation, keyword co-occurrence, and temporal-overlay analyses to examine the intellectual and thematic structure of sustainable waste management and resource recovery. 2.4 Knowledge mapping tools The current study involved the creation and visualization of bibliometric networks using software known as VOSviewer ( www.vosviewer.com ) version 1.6.20 [ 26 ]. Full counting was applied to assign equal weight to each co-occurrence or collaboration link. Association Strength was used as the normalization method to correct differences in occurrence frequencies. Network visualization was generated using a scale of 1.65 visualization. Clusters were identified using a clustering resolution of 1, with a minimum cluster size of 1. For graphical orientation and layout, the degree of rotation was set to 90°. Bibliometric approaches are often used by researchers to identify the significant fields of study, the important aspects of a particular field, and the evolution of publications over time. Bibliometric approaches allow for the visualization of journal-related activities, author-related activities, country-related activities, institutional activities, network activities, word activities, and citation activities [ 29 ]. Every item on the map is presented by means of circles, and the size of each circle is proportional to the frequency or ‘contribution’ of each item. The shading of each circle shows which cluster each item belongs. The thickness and space between each edge shows their strength of connectivity. 2.5 Analytical framework The theoretical framework used in this research has been designed to analyze the intellectual structure of sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems. Several bibliometric techniques have been used to analyze various aspects of research. For example, co-authorship network analysis has been performed to understand co-authorship, co-institutional, and co-country analysis of sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems. Co-citation analysis has been used to understand major references and fundamental research works which form the basis of sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems [ 27 ]. Keyword co-occurrence analysis has been used to understand major themes in the context of sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems. Thematic mapping and keyword word cloud analysis were performed using the Bibliometrix/Biblioshiny tool in R. The thematic map was generated from all keywords based on keyword co-occurrence, highlighting major research themes according to their centrality and density, while the word cloud visualized the most frequently occurring keywords in the dataset [ 32 ]. 3 Results 3.1 Annual publication and citation trends Figure 2 shows the rate of annual publication growth on sustainable waste management and resource recovery systems from 2015 to 2025. The first reference is titled “What a Waste 2.0: A Global Snapshot of Solid Waste Management to 2050” (2018). This report received 280 citations with TLS of 52. It is produced by World Bank and has become one of the leading references due to its comprehensive analysis of solid waste generation and management on a global scale and future forecasts. Second in number is another World Bank study, “What a Waste: A Global Review of Solid Waste Management” (2012) with 139 citations, indicating its prominent position within the intellectual structure of the analyzed literature. Both papers are the ones that have been referenced most in academic literature, indicating the relevance of the global statistics on waste as well as global policies to contemporary studies in the area. In the category of academic literature, one paper that is worth noting is “Conceptualizing the Circular Economy: An Analysis of 114 Definitions” (2017) that has been co-cited 133 times and has a TLS of 82, which indicates its high co-citation prominence within the analyzed network. Another relevant study is “A Review on Circular Economy: The Expected Transition to a Balanced Interplay of Environmental and Economic Systems” (2016) that shows the highest network connectivity (TLS = 88) among all the references. The fourth most co-cited reference is the document entitled “Transforming our world: the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development,” issued by the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (UN SDG) program (2015) and containing 127 citations. The reference chosen for citation shows that the research on sustainable waste management is closely related to the aims of global sustainability. The presence of such policy-oriented literature emphasizes the relevance of waste management activities to other sustainability issues. Methodological and technical references are also present in the list of highly co-cited literature. This growth is consistent with the increasing integration of waste management into broader sustainability and resource-efficiency agendas. The rapid growth of research activity coincides with the implementation of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the widespread adoption of circular economic principles, both of which have accelerated global interest in transforming waste from an environmental burden into a valuable resource. The increase in scientific productivity, together with the growing prominence of recycling, circular economy, resource recovery, water-treatment technologies, and environmental assessment, indicates increasing scholarly attention to approaches that seek to improve resource efficiency and reduce environmental burdens [ 27 ]. While modern technologies like artificial intelligence (AI), Internet of Things (IoT), blockchain technology, and digital monitoring have become more prevalent recently, their role as emerging topics according to the keyword analysis can be traced rather than their dominance [ 17 ]. In contrast, circular economy, waste valorization, biological treatments, and sustainability assessment remain prominent themes in the recent research landscape [ 7 ]. This prevalence of Environmental Science, Engineering, and Energy suggests the cross-disciplinary scope of sustainable waste management research. The interdisciplinary composition of the literature highlights the potential value of integrating technological innovation with environmental assessment, economic considerations, governance, and social acceptance in future waste-management research. Such multidimensionality can be linked to sustainability transition as a general concept, which implies that technological change, institutional change, environmental change, and socio-cultural change should happen simultaneously to increase resource efficiency and environmental sustainability [ 51 , 52 , 53 ]. Increased input from Social Sciences, Business & Management, and Decision Sciences is a clear indication of such awareness. In assessing the contributions of funders and institutions, waste management research is considered strategically important within the context of sustainable national development. The strong representation of Chinese funding agencies and institutions indicates substantial research activity and funding presence in the analyzed dataset, particularly in areas related to circular economy, environmental management, and sustainable urban development. Similarly, the involvement of various European organizations may be associated with initiatives such as the European Green Deal and the Circular Economy Action Plan. It is clear from the above study of the country collaboration network that research on sustainable waste management has become global in nature. Collaborations with countries like China, the US, UK, Italy, and Australia indicate that international cooperation will be required to address the issue of cross-border environmental problems. Factors like knowledge sharing, technology transfer, and collaboration play a significant role in promoting research in sustainable resource recovery techniques [ 52 ]. Nevertheless, substantial geographical disparities remain in scientific and technological capacity. Despite rising research interest at the international level, there is still a significant difference between the capabilities of developed and developing countries regarding waste management and infrastructure. High levels of waste generation in many developing countries are associated with rapid urbanization, industrialization, and population growth [ 52 ]. In contrast, many developing countries face financial constraints, inadequate waste-management infrastructure, limited regulatory capacity, and insufficient treatment facilities [ 2 ]. Considering that, sustainable waste management remains a serious developmental issue in many regions of Asia, Africa, and Latin America, as the presence of unsanitary dumpsites, unmanaged landfills, and other types of unsustainability threatens environmental sustainability and public health. On the contrary, high-income countries have increasingly adopted circular economy principles, sophisticated waste management technologies, and efficient resource recovery approaches [ 54 , 55 , 56 ]. Such different patterns of sustainability transition call for developing targeted solutions for low-income countries that are reflective of their socio-economic context and developmental needs. Clearly, constructing the right infrastructure, enhancing governance structures, offering financial aid, and supporting capacity development would be instrumental in addressing the challenge of waste management in developing countries. The importance of co-citation analysis reveals that the intellectual foundations of the field of study depend heavily on studies related to global waste management perspectives, circular economy theories, methodologies for conducting sustainability assessments, and environmental policy studies [ 27 ]. Since the high citation rates of literature regarding global waste generation, transition into circular economy, and sustainable development are observed, there has been a move toward a holistic perspective of the environment, economy, and society in waste management studies [ 27 ]. The present findings broadly confirm the trends reported in previous bibliometric studies while extending their thematic scope. Khan et al. [ 27 ] identified strong links between waste management and green technologies, while Li et al. [ 28 ] demonstrated the growing research attention to construction and demolition waste management. Yalçıntaş et al. [ 29 ] similarly highlighted the increasing scholarly interest in sustainable waste-management research. The present study extends these contributions by integrating sustainable waste management and resource recovery with circular economy, waste valorization, life-cycle assessment, climate change, and broader sustainability-transition themes within a single analytical framework. Unlike studies focused primarily on a specific waste stream, technology, or thematic subset, the present analysis examines the interconnected intellectual, collaborative, institutional, funding, and thematic structures of the broader research domain. Thus, the findings confirm the previously observed expansion of sustainability-oriented waste research while extending the knowledge-domain perspective to the interrelationships among waste management, resource recovery, circularity, and sustainability transition. Further support for this trend comes from keyword co-occurrences. The fact that concepts such as circular economy, recycling, life cycle assessment, resource recovery, environmental management, climate change, and wastewater treatment appear more frequently suggests that today’s research is becoming more concerned about sustainable practices. The prevalence of anaerobic digestion, composting, biogas generation, and energy-from-waste methods suggests increased interest in waste-to-value systems that can deliver economic and environmental gains [ 57 ]. Such innovations lead to energy production, conservation, and mitigation of greenhouse gas emissions among others. The rising frequency of climate keywords clearly indicates that there is an increasing understanding of the connection between waste management and climate change mitigation. Indeed, waste management can be seen as an aspect of greenhouse gas emissions and at the same time a way to address these issues. On one hand, landfills are still major sources of methane gas emissions, and, on the other hand, the ineffectiveness of waste management leads to environmental degradation and the wasting of resources [ 58 , 59 ]. Moving towards sustainable waste management practices will need to consider the principles of environmental justice, social inclusion, and equitable development. Informal waste collectors play an important role in recycling and resource recovery activities in many developing countries. Many waste collectors and recyclers work under hazardous conditions and face socioeconomic marginalization. Therefore, social dimensions have become an important component of sustainability transitions in waste management [ 60 , 61 , 62 ]. Although adopting innovative technologies of waste management and formalized resource recovery processes have numerous environmental and economic advantages, there may be negative social implications associated with this process because of the exclusion of local populations and informal workers from participation and decision-making processes [ 63 ]. Therefore, waste-management transitions should promote equitable participation, empowerment, and inclusion while strengthening the capacities and livelihoods of informal-sector workers. Integrating the informal sector into formal waste-management systems has been proposed as a strategy that may improve recycling and resource-recovery efficiency while supporting social inclusion [ 62 ]. In addition to illustrating trends in the number of publications and citations, this bibliometric study has also yielded vital theoretical perspectives on the evolution of sustainable waste management studies as part of the environmental development approach. The results suggest a shift in research emphasis from conventional waste-management themes focused on disposal and pollution control toward broader themes encompassing circular economy, climate change, resource recovery, and sustainability transitions. The increasing prominence and interrelation of terms such as circular economy, resource recovery, life-cycle assessment, climate change, and sustainable development suggest that waste is increasingly conceptualized in literature as both an environmental concern and a potential resource. This pattern suggests a broader shift in the research discourse from end-of-pipe treatment toward resource regeneration and more efficient resource use in the development process. The prominence of collaborative networks among China, Europe, and other countries, and the sustainability-related themes indicates that sustainable waste management is increasingly discussed in relation to green growth, environmental resilience, and low-carbon development within the research literature. Theoretically, this study illustrates how waste-management research has evolved into a multidisciplinary knowledge domain integrating environmental, technological, economic, institutional, and social perspectives. Overall, it is evident that sustainable waste management and recycling have emerged as important themes within research addressing environmental development and climate-change adaptation. Future progress in the field may depend on effective governance, institutional capacity, international cooperation, and socially inclusive policies. 4.1 Theoretical and practical implications The findings have several theoretical and practical implications for the development of sustainable waste-management research. The strong interconnections among resource recovery, circular economy, environmental assessment, climate change, governance, and technological innovation support a multidisciplinary conceptualization of the field. These relationships provide a basis for researchers to investigate waste management as part of broader sustainability-transition processes rather than as an isolated waste-treatment problem. For policymakers and funding agencies, the findings highlight research areas relevant to circular-economy, resource-recovery, climate, and environmental objectives and may help inform future research and policy discussions while strengthening international collaboration and research capacity, particularly in developing regions. For industry and technology developers, the growing prominence of waste valorization, resource recovery, waste-to-energy, digital monitoring, AI, and IoT identifies potential areas for innovation. These implications should nevertheless be considered as research priorities rather than evidence of technological or economic effectiveness, which requires independent environmental, life-cycle, and techno-economic assessment. 5 Limitations This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting its findings. First, the analysis was based exclusively on the Scopus database. Although Scopus provides broad multidisciplinary coverage, relevant publications indexed in Web of Science, Dimensions, Google Scholar, or other regional databases may not have been captured. Consequently, the results represent the research landscape available within Scopus rather than the complete global literature. Future studies could strengthen the evidence base through cross-database comparisons. Second, the dataset was restricted to English-language research articles, excluding reviews, book chapters, conference proceedings, editorials, and other document types. This restriction improves bibliographic comparability but may omit relevant conceptual, regional, or emerging research published in other languages or formats. Third, the analysis relies primarily on publication metadata, citation counts, author keywords, and network relationships. Citation frequency reflects scholarly visibility and is influenced by publication age, field-specific citation practices, and other factors; therefore, citation impact should not be interpreted as direct evidence of scientific quality, technological effectiveness, environmental performance, economic feasibility, or policy success. Fourth, although a focused qualitative assessment of five representative publications selected from the 10 most highly cited publications was incorporated to complement the quantitative analysis, this assessment was limited in scope and based on citation prominence rather than a systematic review methodology. It therefore provides contextual interpretation of influential literature but does not represent a comprehensive assessment of the evidence or effectiveness of individual waste-management approaches. Fifth, the thematic findings are derived from keyword co-occurrence and temporal overlay analysis; consequently, thematic prominence and evolution reflect patterns in scholarly terminology and publication activity rather than direct evidence of changes in real-world waste-management practices. Finally, the study identifies research trends, intellectual structures, collaborations, and emerging themes but does not evaluate the actual implementation, performance, or socioeconomic outcomes of specific sustainable waste-management technologies or policies. Future research should therefore complement bibliometric mapping with systematic reviews, comparative case studies, life-cycle assessment, techno-economic evaluation, and empirical evidence from different geographical and socioeconomic contexts to establish how identified research trends translate into practical sustainability outcomes. 6 Future research directions The findings of this bibliometric analysis highlight several priorities for future research on sustainable waste management and resource recovery. In the first place, future research should develop integrated models that incorporate waste-management practices within circular-economy, climate-mitigation, and sustainable-development frameworks. Future studies should evaluate the ability of resource-recovery practices to protect the environment, strengthen economic resilience, and promote social welfare, particularly in rapidly developing countries. Secondly, future research should pay more attention to understanding governance, policymaking, and institution-related aspects of sustainable waste management. Comparative case studies evaluating the impact of existing regulatory measures, economic instruments, public-private collaborations, and community-based management on sustainable waste management systems are needed. This type of research can be especially helpful for those countries where institutional and infrastructural capacities are limited for the introduction of sustainable waste management policies. Third, further attention needs to be devoted to concepts like environmental justice and social inclusion in waste management studies. The next generation of research in this area needs to explore topics such as the significance of informal waste economy, livelihood issues, gender aspects, involvement of local communities, and access to waste management. Such research could help address the fact that this step ensures equity in all developmental efforts towards this economic transition. Fourth, future research should integrate life-cycle assessment, material-flow analysis, and systems approaches to evaluate environmental, economic, and social impacts of resource-recovery pathways. Lastly, while novel technological approaches such as artificial intelligence and IoT technology have the potential to make operational efforts more efficient, it is essential for future researchers to investigate their accessibility and viability across different developmental settings. Interdisciplinary collaboration among environmental scientists, engineers, economists, and sociologists may be important for developing more integrated approaches to sustainable waste management. Future studies could further complement bibliometric mapping with systematic full-text content analysis, machine-learning-assisted thematic classification, and separate assessment of recent influential publications to provide deeper substantive evaluation of the research domain. 7 Conclusion The paper presents a detailed bibliometric review of the global literature on sustainable waste management and resource recovery published between 2015 and 2025. The results indicate a substantial increase in the number of scientific publications in recent years, reflecting growing international interest in sustainable waste management as an essential part of sustainability, environmental protection, resource efficiency, and circular economic development. The growth in scientific activity suggests a shift in research emphasis from conventional waste disposal toward waste valorization, recycling, and resource recovery. The findings reveal that this is a highly interdisciplinary field involving environmental sciences, engineering, energy sciences, sustainable development, and policymaking. Research topics focus on issues such as waste management, recycling, circular economy, wastewater treatment, climate change mitigation, and environmental impact assessments, suggesting an increased interest in the relationship between waste management strategies and sustainability considerations. International collaboration and institutional participation are prominent features of the research landscape in this regard. Although these bibliometric data do not directly measure infrastructure or operational performance, the broader literature identifies persistent disparities between developed and developing regions. Many low- and middle-income countries continue to face challenges associated with rapid urbanization, increasing waste generation, limited financial resources, and inadequate environmental services. Addressing these challenges requires context-specific strategies that integrate environmental, economic, and social considerations. From an environmental development perspective, sustainable waste-management transitions should therefore incorporate environmental justice, social inclusion, and institutional capacity alongside technological and resource-recovery objectives. Overall, the findings provide insights that may help researchers, policymakers, and other stakeholders identify research priorities and areas for further investigation related to circular economy, resource recovery, and sustainable development.
