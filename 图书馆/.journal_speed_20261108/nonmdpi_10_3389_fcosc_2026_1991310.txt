You can register here Notify me Abstract Background: Approximately 40% of the world's 7,000 languages are endangered, and language loss may erode traditional ecological knowledge (TEK) that supports biodiversity stewardship. No integrative synthesis has examined how language vitality and language of instruction (LOI) jointly relate to sustainability-relevant knowledge, cognition, and practice. Aim: This narrative review synthesises empirical, theoretical, and policy literature on these relationships and proposes the Language-Ecology-Sustainability Interface (LESI) Model, distinguishing what sources establish, what the model proposes, and what remains to be tested. Methods: Purposive searches of Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed Central (prioritising January 2020– August 2026) were supplemented by citation tracking and a search for disconfirming evidence. Studies were selected by explicit rules, appraised narratively against six domains, and mapped to the model link each measures. Coverage is not exhaustive. Results: First, over 75% of documented medicinal plant services in a large sample were linguistically unique, linking language endangerment to TEK vulnerability; this concerns one link and does not test effects on behaviour or biodiversity. Second, a systematic review including 11 randomised trials suggests that mother-tongue LOI may improve literacy, with effects varying across interventions and outcomes; no controlled study of LOI and environmental outcomes was identified. Third, cultural change and market integration co-occur with TEK decline, although some communities retain knowledge. Indigenous-managed lands overlap substantially with protected areas and intact landscapes; this evidence concerns land tenure, not language. Fourth, linguistic and biological diversity co-occur geographically. Conclusions: The evidence is mostly cross-sectional, confounded by land tenure and market integration, geographically uneven, and appraised narratively, so the reported relationships are associations. The LESI pathways are proposed mechanisms; none has been tested end-to-end. Policy proposals, including language vitality as a biodiversity indicator, are offered for evaluation under stated decision rules and ethical safeguards, not for implementation at scale.
