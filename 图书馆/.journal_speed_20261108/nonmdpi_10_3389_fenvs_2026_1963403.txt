School of International Law, Zhongnan University of Economics and Law, Wuhan, Hubei, China See more Article metrics View details Abstract Introduction: Whole-region comprehensive land consolidation (WRCLC), as a spatial governance approach integrating “all elements, all regions, the whole process, and the entire chain,” is of great significance for optimizing regional production–living–ecological space (PLES) functions. Methods: Existing studies have not yet systematically examined, from the holistic perspective of PLES functions, the spatial network mechanism and safeguard pathways of WRCLC at the macro level. Therefore, based on township-level panel data from Zhejiang Province for 2015–2022, this study comprehensively employs a multi-period difference-in-differences (DID) model, social network analysis (SNA), and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to systematically investigate the network mechanism and institutional safeguard pathways through which the WRCLC pilot policy enhances PLES functions. Results: The results show that the WRCLC pilot policy significantly improved the comprehensive PLES function (by 0.0023 units); this finding is significant at the 1% level and remains robust after a series of robustness checks. Meanwhile, network mechanism analysis reveals that the policy indirectly enhances PLES functions by improving the spatial correlation network. Configuration analysis further identifies three differentiated pathways: the “capital–industry” synergy type, the “human–capital”-driven type, and the multi-element synergy type, revealing the multiple concurrent conditions and factor combination logic underlying policy effectiveness. Discussion: Based on these findings, it is recommended that governments, when promoting WRCLC, should pay attention to regional spatial structure and factor endowment differences, strengthen rule-of-law safeguards, and implement differentiated governance strategies to achieve holistic optimization and coordinated development of PLES functions. 1 Introduction Territorial space is the fundamental carrier of human production and living practices and ecological development. The degree of coordination among production, living, and ecological spatial functions (hereinafter referred to as the “PLES functions”) directly affects the quality and resilience of regional sustainable development. However, rapid urbanization and industrialization worldwide continue to reshape territorial space use patterns. Data from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) show that between 2001 and 2023, global agricultural land area decreased by 78 million hectares1, while built-up land expansion far outpaced population growth; land degradation affects approximately 40% of the global terrestrial area2. Against this backdrop, land consolidation, as a core policy instrument for optimizing territorial spatial allocation and addressing urban–rural development imbalances, has been widely implemented and has continued to evolve across countries. Germany, with the Flurbereinigungsgesetz (Land Consolidation Act) as its core, has formed a comprehensive land consolidation legal system covering the adjustment of land rights, parcel consolidation, public facility construction, and rural development3. Japan, through laws such as the Land Improvement Act4, the Act on Improvement of Agricultural Promotion Areas5, and the Land Readjustment Act6, has constructed land use and consolidation systems from aspects including the improvement of agricultural infrastructure, the protection of agricultural land and agricultural land use planning, and the reallocation of urban and rural land, respectively. Turkey, Law No. 3083 on Land Reform regarding Rearrangement of Land in Irrigated Areas7; Law No. 5403 on Soil Preservation and Land Utilization8, integrates land consolidation, land reallocation, agricultural structural adjustment, cultivated land protection, and land use control. The evolution of the land consolidation legal systems in the above countries indicates that land consolidation has gradually evolved from early engineering-oriented parcel adjustment into a composite governance tool integrating spatial planning, tenure reorganization, ecological restoration, and community development. Since the 18th National Congress of the Communist Party of China, China has attached great importance to the protection and development of territorial space and has continuously strengthened top-level design. The Fourth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee of the Communist Party of China further explicitly proposed that it is necessary to “coordinate the advancement of county-level territorial space governance and steadily carry out comprehensive land consolidation across the entire area,” so as to build a regional economic layout and a territorial space system characterized by complementary advantages and high-quality development. The Land Administration Law of the People’s Republic of China establishes the basic norms for land use control, territorial spatial planning, cultivated land protection, and land consolidation9. Article 42 of the Law stipulates that the state encourages land consolidation; county and township (town) people’s governments shall organize rural collective economic organizations to carry out comprehensive consolidation of fields, water, roads, forests, and villages in accordance with the overall land use plan, so as to improve the quality of cultivated land, increase the effective area of cultivated land, and improve agricultural production conditions and the ecological environment; at the same time, the Law also provides for the obligation of land reclamation. Regulations for the Implementation of the Land Administration Law of the People’s Republic of China further refine the relevant systems of territorial spatial planning implementation10, cultivated land protection, land use control, and land reclamation. On the basis of the basic systems of land management, territorial spatial planning, and ecological protection established by existing laws and regulations, the national level has further, through the Guidelines for the Implementation of Comprehensive Land Consolidation (Trial), made detailed provisions on the implementation units, consolidation areas, and implementation plans, and project arrangements of comprehensive land consolidation. Localities have also formed corresponding implementation norms in light of their own resource conditions and governance needs11. The above laws, administrative regulations, and policy implementation documents no longer position land consolidation merely as a technical activity related to land use efficiency or agricultural production, but instead place it within the interrelated legal governance objectives of land use control, cultivated land protection, territorial spatial planning, rural revitalization, and ecological restoration. At the local level, Zhejiang Province was the first to explore and advance comprehensive land consolidation integrating production, living, and ecological functions. Through whole-area planning and design, it rationally allocates agricultural land, construction land, and ecological land, aiming to systematically resolve the “zero-sum dilemma” between development and protection (Yu et al., 2021). Zhejiang Province has enacted dedicated Land Consolidation Regulations of Zhejiang Province, which systematically provide for the concept, planning, implementation, supervision and management, and legal liability of land consolidation12. These Regulations define land consolidation as the consolidation, reclamation, and development of unused or unreasonably used land for the purpose of increasing the effective area of cultivated land and improving the quality of cultivated land, and require that land consolidation adhere to the priority of ecological and environmental protection and safeguard the rights of land right holders to information, participation, and supervision. In addition, Article 36 of the Territorial Spatial Planning Regulations of Zhejiang Province further provides that county (city, district) people’s governments shall, in accordance with the requirements of territorial spatial planning, formulate land comprehensive consolidation plans and, through the implementation of land consolidation, village improvement, and consolidation of inefficient urban land, systematically advance the comprehensive consolidation of fields, water, roads, forests, and villages, so as to promote the optimization of territorial spatial layout and the development of industrial agglomeration13. Thus, local regulations have incorporated comprehensive land consolidation into the institutional framework of territorial spatial planning implementation and economical and intensive land use. At the same time, laws and administrative regulations such as the Law of the People’s Republic of China on Urban and Rural Planning14, Law of the People’s Republic of China on the Promotion of Rural Revitalization15, and the Regulation on Land Reclamation16 provide supplementary normative bases for land consolidation from aspects including urban and rural spatial planning, rural ecological governance, and the restoration of damaged land. As a result, comprehensive land consolidation has formed a multi-level institutional arrangement ranging from laws and regulations to policy implementation rules and then to specific local practices. However, because the specific requirements of norms at different levels still differ in terms of planning articulation, departmental coordination, project implementation, and interest coordination, the implementation of comprehensive land consolidation still exhibits strong local institutional dependence. At present, comprehensive land consolidation has moved from the pilot stage to a stage of in-depth nationwide advancement, becoming a key platform for implementing territorial spatial planning and an important institutional vehicle for advancing the modernization of spatial governance. Therefore, establishing a scientific evaluation system and exploring the mechanisms and enhancement pathways through which pilot policies for comprehensive land consolidation affect the “PLES functions” have important policy reference value for promoting the sustainable development of territorial space. Early studies mostly explored the theoretical logic of comprehensive land consolidation from a qualitative perspective, focusing on the interaction between land consolidation and spatial restructuring. Related studies have pointed out that comprehensive land consolidation promotes spatial restructuring through measures such as agricultural land consolidation, construction land consolidation, and ecological protection and restoration, exerting systemic effects on rural production, living, and ecological spaces (Gao et al., 2024). Some studies have used micro-survey data and case analysis to preliminarily reveal the impact of comprehensive land consolidation on production. Research shows that this policy significantly promotes land transfer and facilitates the transformation of agricultural organizational and operational modes, agricultural business scope, and agricultural production methods (Huanxin et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2026). However, other studies have found that comprehensive land consolidation still largely follows the traditional land consolidation approach of “emphasizing tasks, engineering, and indicators,” which cannot meet the goals of sustainable development and runs counter to the long-term objectives of rural revitalization (Jin et al., 2022). As an important indicator of regional sustainable development, the coordination of “production-living-ecological” spatial functions has irreplaceable value for systematically evaluating policy effects. In addition, some studies have noted that the integration relationship among “production-living-ecological” spaces is no longer limited to the traditional linear model but exhibits complex spatial network structural characteristics (Xiang et al., 2025). Therefore, there is an urgent need to systematically discuss the network mechanisms and enhancement pathways of comprehensive land consolidation at the macro level from the perspective of “production-living-ecological” spatial functions. Meanwhile, the relationships among “production-living-ecological” spatial functions are no longer confined to the traditional linear coupling model but present complex spatial network structural characteristics. Empirical studies at the metropolitan area scale (Yuan et al., 2024) show that the spatial correlation network of “production-living-ecological” functions has significant core–periphery structure and polycentric nested characteristics, and factor flows and functional spillover effects are transmitted among townships through transportation corridors, industrial chains, and ecological networks. This finding imposes new requirements on the policy design of comprehensive land consolidation, namely, consolidation activities should not only focus on optimizing factor allocation within a single township but also attend to cross-township spatial correlation effects and functional synergy mechanisms. However, existing studies remain unclear about how comprehensive land consolidation affects “production-living-ecological” spatial functions through spatial network mechanisms at the macro level (Xu et al., 2025). Moreover, at the institutional level, some studies have found that the implementation effectiveness of comprehensive land consolidation is highly dependent on local institutional arrangements and factor combinations. Different resource endowments, staffing arrangements, and governance conditions can lead to differentiated governance pathways and ultimately produce different policy effects (Zhang et al., 2026). Existing studies mostly focus on the independent influence of a single institutional factor on consolidation outcomes (Jing et al., 2024) or summarize typical models based on case experience (Tong et al., 2025), but rarely analyze, from a holistic perspective, the concurrent effects of the interactions among multiple institutional and resource factors on the improvement of “production-living-ecological” spatial functions. As a result, they struggle to explain the sources of differences in policy effects under different factor endowment conditions. Based on the gaps in existing research, this study takes townships in Zhejiang Province that were among the earliest to carry out comprehensive land consolidation pilots as the research object, uses township-level panel data from 2015 to 2022, systematically evaluates the net effect of the pilot policy on improving “production-living-ecological” spatial functions, further identifies the spatial network mechanisms through which the policy operates, and, through configurational analysis, summarizes and distills institutional safeguard pathways under different factor conditions, so as to provide empirical support and theoretical reference for optimizing the policy design of comprehensive land consolidation in China (see Figure 1). FIGURE 1 Theoretical analysis framework. 2 Theoretical analysis 2.1 The direct impact of the WRCLC pilot policy on PLES functions WRCLC uses townships as the basic implementation unit, taking into account diverse regional factors and the coordination and integration of departments and human-land relationships. It involves the consolidation of agricultural land, construction land, and rural ecological protection and restoration, making it a crucial platform for land spatial planning and a strong spatial support for rural revitalization (Zhou et al., 2024). At the broader spatial level, WRCLC is grounded in the theory of spatial production and governance modernization. Through scientific planning and systematic rectification, it promotes the reconstruction and optimization of national land space. It breaks the fragmented model of traditional land consolidation and realizes the coordinated arrangement and rational layout of agricultural, construction, and ecological land, thus enhancing the integrated benefits of the PLES functions. In the dimension of production space, the policy, based on resource allocation theory, not only directly enhances the quality of cultivated land and agricultural productivity through agricultural land consolidation, but also provides critical spatial support for the integration and upgrading of rural industries through the revitalization and reuse of construction land, thereby activating endogenous development momentum. In the transformation of living space, the policy profoundly reflects human-centered development and livability. By improving infrastructure and public services while emphasizing the protection of rural landscapes and cultural context during the consolidation process, it achieves an organic unity of modern living convenience and the unique charm of rural areas. Finally, in the ecological dimension, the policy implements landscape ecology and sustainable development theories. Through systematic protection and restoration projects and the construction of ecological networks, it enhances the stability and service functions of ecosystems, ensuring ecological security and biodiversity. In conclusion, WRCLC is guided by a systematic theoretical framework, achieving the comprehensive governance goal of synergizing the PLES functions Accordingly, this study derives its first hypothesis: H1: The WRCLC pilot policy positively influences the functions of the PLES. 2.2 The network mechanism of the WRCLC pilot policy on PLES functions Based on SNA theory (Li et al., 2025) and the practice of WRCLC, this policy can reshape the structural relationships among the PLES functions within a region, thereby promoting their functional enhancement. Specifically, core indicators such as degree centrality, closeness centrality, and betweenness centrality can be employed for explanation, corresponding respectively to the network centrality, connectivity, and “bridge” roles of pilot townships PLES functions within the entire region (Su et al., 2025). Firstly, in terms of degree centrality, the WRCLC policy does not target the fragmented improvement of individual plots or functions. Instead, it undertakes integrated planning and holistic optimization of PLES. According to the “Multi-Plan Integration (MPI)” theory (Zhao and Li, 2023), this approach can break the original fragmented and isolated spatial utilization patterns, promoting a transition of township land use functions from singular to synergistic. Such functional strengthening and optimization enhance the output capacity and attractiveness of pilot townships in the regional PLES functions network, whether as suppliers of production factors, providers of ecological services, or carriers of livable spaces. Consequently, the “connection channels” for element flows, functional complementarities, and spatial linkages with other townships are significantly increased. This is reflected in a growth in the number of direct connections within the network, namely, an effective increase in degree centrality, thereby moving the township from the periphery to the center of the network and establishing it as a key node for the circulation of PLES functions. Secondly, in terms of closeness centrality, one of the core tasks of WRCLC is to construct an efficient and coordinated land space structure, which directly influences the cost and efficiency of spatial connections. Through the implementation of land ownership adjustments, integration of transportation infrastructure, and the construction of ecological networks, this policy can significantly reduce spatial barriers both within and outside rural towns. According to the theory of spatial accessibility (Lloyd, 2023), the elimination of spatial barriers means that the physical distance and cost distance for functional transmission from the pilot rural town to any other node in the network are shortened. Meanwhile, the shorter the path, the more direct the connection. As a result, the pilot rural town’s dependence on intermediate nodes for accessing resources, radiating functions, and exerting influence becomes lower, enhancing its capacity for independent action and rapid response. Therefore, WRCLC improves spatial connectivity efficiency, bringing the pilot rural town closer to a core position of “accessibility in all directions”, thereby increasing its closeness centrality. Thirdly, in terms of betweenness centrality, WRCLC can significantly enhance the intermediary centrality of the pilot area’s PLES functions by shaping regional functional hubs, thereby strengthening its strategic position in controlling the flow of resource information. The macro effect of comprehensive consolidation lies in reshaping the spatial functional structure of the region, which often leads to the pilot rural town undertaking specific functional roles, such as agricultural industrial zones, ecological service zones, etc. When pilot townships leverage core functions that have been strengthened or newly introduced through governance interventions to connect different parts or functional modules within the network, previously low-efficiency nodes may experience intensified interactions due to their “bridging” role. This allows the pilot rural town to gain powerful connectivity in the PLES functional network, enabling it to influence or even control the flow paths and efficiency of resources, information, and energy between other towns, effectively increasing its intermediary centrality. Accordingly, this study derives its second hypothesis: H2: WRCLC helps promote the enhancement of the spatial correlation network of PLES functions. 2.3 The configuration framework of the WRCLC pilot policy and PLES functions Grounded in the theory of the regional man-land relationship system (Jin et al., 2020), the interaction between humans and land is evidently multi-element and complex, involving the interconnection of factors such as population, land, and economy. A systematic analysis approach is required to represent the underlying mechanisms of this relationship. The configurational perspective, with its focus on the phenomena of “multiple conjunctural causation” and “equifinality”, can effectively explain the non-linear relationships and causal asymmetries in complex systems. This perspective is particularly useful in exploring the multi-element combinatory paths through which comprehensive land consolidation impacts the spatial functions of the PLES. In this context, this paper adopts the configurational perspective to categorize the various factors in the human-environment system into core and peripheral systems, and develops a theoretical framework to analyze the synergy between the “human–land–industry” and “finance–governance–rights” dimensions. This framework systematically explains the diverse pathways through which comprehensive land consolidation promotes the enhancement of the PLES functions (Luo et al., 2024). The core system, comprising the three fundamental elements of “human–land–industry” (Xu and Zhang, 2023; Sun and Lu, 2023; He, 2018), interacts synergistically to constitute the endogenous driving force behind the evolution of regional spatial functions. Among them, “human” are the principal actors and key decision-makers of spatial demand, as the pursuit of livability drives settlement restructuring. Additionally, rational economic agents, as represented by new types of agricultural business entities and rural entrepreneurs, can proactively identify policy opportunities and lead the rectification practices. By optimizing land use decisions, they directly affect the efficiency of production spaces and the configuration of ecological spaces. The “land” element is the material carrier of spatial functions and the core object of rectification efforts. By implementing projects like farmland rectification, construction land reorganization, and ecological restoration, it directly reshapes the physical form, utilization structure, and functional relationships of the PLES. The “industry” element serves as the economic engine that stimulates spatial vitality. Rectification efforts guide the optimization of industrial spatial layouts, promote agricultural scale management, and rural industrial integration (RII). This injects continuous momentum into production spaces and supports the sustainability of improved living quality and ecological protection through industrial linkages. The peripheral system provides critical external support for the operation of the core system, encompassing three key elements: “finance,” “governance,” and “rights.” The “finance” element is the material foundation for advancing the governance process. By relying on financial tools such as special government funds, private capital introduction, and ecological compensation, a diversified funding pool is created to ensure the enhancement of the PLES functions. The “governance” element serves as the organizational hub for coordinated progress. Efficient governance conditions can effectively implement WRCLC policy across the region, promote the participation of multiple stakeholders in project development, and establish a strong endogenous driving mechanism. This creates a stable and favorable external environment and internal driving force for the advancement of policy. The “rights” element is the institutional core for stimulating internal motivation. By clarifying the market-based transaction system and ensuring the rights and interests of farmers as key stakeholders in land consolidation, their enthusiasm and creativity for participating in the governance process are stimulated, thus providing institutional guarantees for the enhancement of the PLES functions. From a configurational perspective, the core system and the peripheral system do not exist in isolation but are intertwined and synergize to collectively constitute a complex system through which WRCLC enhances PLES functions. On the one hand, the “human-land-industry” elements of the core system interact with and feedback on each other, forming the endogenous driving force for the evolution of regional spatial functions, providing target direction and practical foundation for the operation of the peripheral system. On the other hand, the “finance-governance-rights” elements of the peripheral system provide material support, organizational guarantees, and institutional incentives, creating favorable conditions for the operation of the core system and promoting its development to a higher level. This synergistic interaction between the core and peripheral systems enables WRCLC to apply multiple elements and means, achieving the systematic enhancement and optimization of the PLES functions. Accordingly, this study derives its third hypothesis: H3: WRCLC promotes the enhancement of PLES functions through the synergistic coordination of “human-land-industry” and “finance-governance-rights” elements. 3 Materials and methods 3.1 Overview of the study area Zhejiang Province is located in the southern wing of the Yangtze River Delta on the southeast coast of China, spanning 27°02′–31°11′N and 118°01′–123°10′E. It has a monsoon-influenced humid climate with concurrent rainfall and heat. The province has a land area of 105,500 km2, of which mountains account for 74.6%, water surfaces 5.1%, and flat land 20.3%. It is known as a region of “seven parts mountains, one part water, and two parts farmland,” and the contradiction between economic development and scarce land resources is prominent. Since 2018, Zhejiang Province has taken the lead in carrying out comprehensive land consolidation pilots, announcing three batches of township-level pilots in 2019, 2020, and 2021, respectively. Among them, the first and second batches of provincial-level pilots for comprehensive land consolidation and ecological restoration, announced on 6 June 2019 and 11 September 2020, totaled 497, while the 42 national-level pilots announced by the Ministry of Natural Resources in 2021 brought the total to 539. After excluding street-level pilots, samples with severe missing data, and samples affected by administrative division adjustments, only 4,312 valid observations were ultimately obtained (see Figure 2). Instead, it arises from the interplay and coordinated effects of various consolidation factors across different settings. Traditional econometric methods have the advantage of identifying the “net effect” of individual factors but struggle to reveal the concurrency of multiple factors, the substitution and complementarity between elements, as well as intricate causal mechanisms such as “equifinality”. To address these theoretical challenges, this study employs fsQCA for in-depth analysis (Wu et al., 2026). FsQCA is a case-oriented analytical technique well-suited for handling complex causal relationships in small to medium-sized samples. It allows each region implementing comprehensive land consolidation to be viewed as a complex case, where systematic comparison can detect distinct arrangements of intervention elements that lead to high or non-high levels of PLES functions, thereby revealing the causal pathways. In this context, this paper further selects pilot samples to explore the configuration logic behind the policy’s effectiveness, aiming to clarify the pathways through which the “core-periphery” system enhances PLES functions. 3.4 Variable selection 3.4.1 Dependent variable This study takes PLES functions—namely production, living, and ecological functions—as the dependent variable. Possible reasons are as follows: First, comprehensive land consolidation takes townships as the basic implementation unit, and administrative boundaries and assessment units remain relatively strong, so cross-township resource allocation and joint consolidation projects are relatively limited. Second, the policy implementation period is relatively short, and the formation of intermediary nodes and bridging functions requires longer-term network evolution and project accumulation. Third, multiple alternative paths exist in the PLES network, and a few non-pilot central towns may assume intermediary functions, resulting in insignificant changes in the betweenness centrality of pilot townships. Fourth, PLES improvement relies more on factor flows brought by direct connections and accessibility than on “control” over network paths. Combined with the effect decomposition of the spatial Durbin model, the direct, indirect, and total effects of the policy are all significantly positive, indicating that the policy improves PLES mainly through local direct improvement and neighborhood spillovers rather than through transmission via intermediary nodes. Network centrality was highly concentrated in townships of the northern Hangzhou Bay area, including Yuyao, Cixi, Zhenhai, and Beilun, where centrality values reached the range of 18.001–21.000, forming a strong agglomeration and radiation pole of economic activity. In stark contrast, most townships in the southern counties of Ninghai and Xiangshan remained on the network periphery, with centrality values mostly below 13.000 and weak connections. At this stage, high-value areas of PLES functions were strictly confined to the northern core, indicating a tight coupling between network structure and the stage of economic growth. A significant development gradient and functional fragmentation existed between land and sea, and between the north and the south. By 2020, during the rollout of the pilot policy, driven by Ningbo’s WRCLC pilot program characterised by “systemic reconfiguration”, the network structure began to shift toward multi-polarisation and maritime integration. The absolute dominance of the northern core persisted, but its radiation range and connection intensity started to expand in multiple directions. A key change was the notable increase in the centrality of coastal townships with good port resources, such as Hepu and Shipu in Xiangshan County, which rose to a higher hierarchical level, signalling that the value of maritime space had been activated through consolidation. The network structure began to evolve from a single “inland economic monopole” toward a preliminary “land-sea coordination” configuration, with medium-to-high-value areas of spatial functionality showing a scattered diffusion trend along transport corridors and the coastline. By 2022, in the post-policy implementation period, Ningbo’s spatial network had evolved into a mature pattern characterised by “one core leading and three zones interacting”. The northern urban-economic core continued to play a leading role, but the drivers of the network became more diversified. The eastern coastal area, leveraging the integration of port resources and upgrading of port-side industries, developed a distinct maritime economic zone. The southern area, through ecological restoration and protection combined with the development of green industries, upgraded from an ecological “base” to a transformational “hub”. The western Siming Mountain area, serving as a critical ecological barrier, saw its structural function further clarified within the network. Corresponding to this multi-polar network, high-value areas of PLES functions exhibited a “scattered but well-distributed” pattern. This indicates that WRCLC, through systematic functional reshaping and spatial reorganisation, effectively broke the original path dependence, promoted a profound transition from a single “economically monopole-driven” to a “land–sea multi-element symbiosis”, and enhanced the overall resilience and collaborative efficiency of regional development. 5.3.2 Analysis of network structure evolution in Jiaxing City In 2015, before the WRCLC pilot policy was rolled out, the PLES network in Jiaxing City exhibited a pronounced pattern of “single-pole concentration, stronger north and weaker south”. Network centrality was highly concentrated in townships located in northern Jiashan County and northeastern Pinghu City, adjacent to the border with Shanghai. Leveraging their geographical and industrial advantages, these areas developed strong regional radiation and factor agglomeration capacities. In contrast, townships in Haiyan County, southern Haining City, and western Tongxiang City remained at the network’s periphery, with generally low centrality and weak spatial connectivity. At this stage, high-value areas of PLES functions closely overlapped with the network core, indicating that the level of economic development was the dominant factor determining network position, and the overall network structure was unbalanced. By 2020, during the pilot implementation phase of the policy, with the advancement of consolidation projects such as “consolidating fragmented plots” and improving farmland quality and contiguity, the network structure began to undergo systemic reconfiguration. A notable change was the significant increase in centrality among townships in Tongxiang City and Haining City with strong agricultural endowments, which gradually evolved into emerging secondary cores. This shift directly resulted from the optimization of agricultural production space through land consolidation: large-scale, well-equipped farmland enhanced the nodal roles of these townships in regional agricultural supply and rural landscape systems. The network pattern transitioned from a “single-core” to a “dual-core” configuration, and high-value areas of spatial functionality correspondingly spread from north to south, demonstrating the initial effectiveness of policy intervention in reshaping the network structure. The area centered on the “Tianfu Ten-Thousand-Mu Contiguous Farmland” zone emerged as a key growth pole in the network, with its centrality rising to among the highest in the city, standing alongside the original industrial core. Network connections became highly dense and balanced, and the earlier north–south gradient disparity largely disappeared. High- and medium-high-value areas of PLES functions became extensively contiguous, forming functional improvement corridors that spanned the municipal territory. This evolutionary trajectory clearly demonstrates that WRCLC—focused on arable land integration and quality enhancement—can systematically cultivate new network hubs by optimizing the production space carrier, effectively promoting the transformation of regional spatial networks from “single-pole dependence” to “multi-pole synergy”, and achieving coordinated improvements in PLES functions across the region. 6 Configuration path analysis 6.1 Data calibration This study used fsQCA to investigate the intricate causal links that are both necessary and sufficient underlying the improvement of PLES functions, focusing on the interaction between “human–land–industry” factor configurations and “finance–governance–rights” mechanism configurations. A typical case of this pathway is Linghu Town, Nanxun District, Huzhou City. Nanxun District innovatively established a state-owned enterprise, the Strong Village and Prosperous People Group, to consolidate scattered fishponds across villages into contiguous areas. It invested more than 90 million yuan to build a digital modern fishery park and attracted young professionals specializing in aquaculture to contract fishponds and start businesses, effectively enhancing spatial functions and industrial resilience. 6.3.2 Configurational path analysis of non-high production–living–ecological space functions Finance shortage emerges as a pervasive bottleneck leading to poor functionality, appearing in all non-high configurations and constituting a fundamental cause of functional failure. Furthermore, these configurations exhibit two typical types of predicament. The first is a “dual factor deficiency” type, in which a lack of financial resources coincides with the absence of other core development factors such as land or industry, resulting in severely inadequate systemic support. The second is the “rights–land failure” type, characterised by the presence of land and rights mechanisms against a backdrop of capital scarcity, yet these mechanisms fail to effectively integrate and steer the remaining resources, and may even exacerbate system-level inefficiencies due to misalignment. This suggests that non-high levels of PLES functions do not stem from the absence of a single condition, but rather from a “failure assemblage” formed between capital shortages and other factors or governance mechanisms. 6.4 Robustness test This study conducted a robustness test of the “core-periphery” system configurations that generate high levels of PLES functions. Following established practices, the raw consistency threshold for cases was raised from 0.8 to 0.85 (White et al., 2021). A comparison of the results showed that the configurations of antecedent conditions remained identical. This robustness test confirms the robustness of the above findings. 7 Discussion and policy implications 7.1 Discussion Using a township-level panel dataset for Zhejiang Province covering 2015–2022, this study comprehensively investigates the network mechanism and enhancement pathways of WRCLC on PLES by integrating the multi-period DID method, SNA, and fsQCA. Through theoretical development and empirical testing, the key findings are summarized below. First, the WRCLC pilot policy has a significant positive effect on PLES functions. Second, the policy improves the overall performance of PLES functions by increasing the degree centrality and closeness centrality of townships within the spatial correlation network of PLES. Third, the influence of the policy relies on the systematic interaction of the “core–periphery” system. The main enhancement pathways include the “capital–industry synergy” type, the “human–capital” driven type, and the “multi-element synergy” type. These pathways reveal, from different perspectives, the multiple condition combinations through which the policy objectives are achieved, thereby offering theoretical insights and practical guidance for differentially promoting WRCLC to enhance PLES functions. 7.2 Policy implications First, the pilot work of comprehensive land consolidation should be continuously advanced and the policy implementation mechanism improved. It is recommended that, on the basis of summarizing pilot experience, a whole-process governance system of “planning–implementation–supervision” be established, and that the benefit distribution mechanism of land consolidation be improved to ensure that policy benefits are sustained. At the same time, at the legislative level of territorial spatial planning, rule-of-law safeguards and institutional coordination should be strengthened, the legislative process of the Territorial Spatial Planning Law should be accelerated, the legal relationship between comprehensive land consolidation and the territorial spatial planning system should be clarified, and comprehensive land consolidation should be incorporated into the implementation mechanism of territorial spatial planning. Where it is truly necessary to adjust planning during the implementation of consolidation, the corresponding trigger conditions, justification requirements, review bodies, and approval procedures should be further refined, and requirements for linking the adjusted planning results with the consolidation implementation plan should be stipulated. At the level of specialized legislation on land consolidation, the Land Consolidation Regulations may be revised to refine the technical standards for linking consolidation projects with spatial planning, and to stipulate that a spatial compliance review must be conducted before a consolidation project is approved, and that projects failing the review shall not be approved. The current Guidelines for the Implementation of Comprehensive Land Consolidation (Trial) already provide relatively specific policy guidance on implementation plans, project management, monitoring and supervision, and the protection of people’s rights and interests. On the basis of summarizing practical experience, the institutional arrangements of general applicability therein can be further transformed into stable administrative regulation rules, while specific technical standards and operational requirements can be left to departmental rules and local implementation norms. In response to the differences in local institutional conditions revealed by this study, the boundaries of responsibilities, coordination procedures, and accountability of departments such as natural resources, agriculture and rural affairs, ecological environment, and housing and urban-rural development at different stages of projects should be clarified, so as to reduce implementation deviations caused by departmental fragmentation and differences in local governance capacity. Second, attention should be paid to spatial network development and regional synergistic efficiency should be enhanced. Policy implementation should focus on the structural position of townships within the regional network. By improving degree centrality and closeness centrality, a cross-regional platform for sharing resource factors should be established to promote the synergistic optimization of “production–living–ecological” (PLES) functions across a broader area. At the same time, long-term operation of the coordination mechanism should be guaranteed through legal means. Sections 13 (3) and Article 14 of Germany’s Federal Spatial Planning Act (Raumordnungsgesetz)17, through mechanisms such as joint regional planning, cross-regional planning cooperation, and regional networks, provide an institutionalized coordination pathway for coordinating spatial development that has close spatial linkages but extends beyond a single administrative region. This Act is not specialized legislation on comprehensive land consolidation, but the planning cooperation and coordination mechanisms it establishes for cross-administrative-region spaces have reference value for China in improving the regional coordination mechanism in comprehensive land consolidation. In light of China’s existing practices, the boundaries of rights and responsibilities across regions and departments should be further clarified in accordance with the law, and mechanisms for planning articulation, information sharing, interest coordination, and accountability should be improved, so that spatial network development and regional collaborative governance have a legal basis and operate stably, thereby enhancing overall regional synergistic efficiency. Third, differentiated advancement strategies should be implemented. Each locality should choose suitable pathways according to its resource endowments to achieve an overall improvement in “production–living–ecological” (PLES) functions across the entire area. In this process, ecological priority and rule-of-law safeguards should be upheld. In territorial spatial planning, ecological function zoning techniques should be introduced. With the help of ecological sensitivity assessment, ecological carrying capacity analysis, and land ecological suitability evaluation, ecological protection zones, agricultural production zones, urban development zones, and ecological buffer zones should be scientifically delineated, and development intensity in different zones should be strictly controlled. The management and control of ecological protection red lines, permanent basic farmland, and urban development boundaries should be strengthened to ensure that land development conforms to the principle of ecological priority. At the same time, laws and regulations related to land resource protection should be improved. The “Avoid, Reduce, Compensate” (ERC) sequence established in France’s Environmental Code requires that environmental impacts be avoided with priority during planning and project implementation, that mitigation measures be taken for impacts that cannot be avoided, and that ecological compensation be implemented for residual impacts that still remain; for environmental impacts that cannot be adequately addressed through avoidance, mitigation, or compensation, the relevant projects shall not be permitted under the original plan18. This system places ecological protection requirements prior to development decisions and extends ecological impact control to the project implementation stage through compensation responsibilities, providing reference value for China in improving pre-development ecological assessment, environmental impact assessment, and ecological compensation mechanisms in comprehensive land consolidation. On this basis, China should further strengthen the environmental impact assessment system, strictly implement pre-development ecological assessment and environmental compensation mechanisms, increase penalties for illegal ecological destruction, and ensure that differentiated advancement pathways always operate within the track of the rule of law and the bottom line of ecology, thereby promoting the sustainable development and utilization of land resources.
