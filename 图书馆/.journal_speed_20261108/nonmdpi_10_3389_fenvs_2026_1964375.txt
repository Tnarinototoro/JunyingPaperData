You can register here Notify me Abstract As living standards continue to rise, reducing household consumption-based carbon emissions through changes in the consumption structure is essential for reconciling consumption upgrading with the green and low-carbon transition of household living. Using China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) data from 2014 to 2022, this study applies kernel density estimation, consistent partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLSc-SEM), and XGBoost-SEM to examine the spatiotemporal differentiation, category composition, and driving relationships of household consumption-based carbon emissions in the Yangtze River Economic Belt. The findings are as follows: first, aggregate emissions from the sampled households increased, subsequently declined, and then rebounded across the survey waves. Residential emissions accounted for 71.94%–80.28% of the total, while residential, food-related, and transportation and communication emissions jointly accounted for 90.3%–95.6%. Provincial means for the three major categories were generally higher in the downstream region, although Guizhou and Hunan in the middle and upper reaches also exhibited local high values for residential and transportation and communication emissions. Second, household economic conditions were the principal carbon-increasing correlate in the linear model, with net linear effects of 0.440, 0.693, and 0.695 on residential, food-related, and transportation and communication emissions, respectively. Consumption structure upgrading showed a negative linear relationship with residential emissions, while its nonlinear predictive contribution differed in direction across food-related and transportation and communication emissions. Third, the direction of educational investment differed between the linear path estimates and the nonlinear predictive contributions across the three major consumption categories, while digital infrastructure also showed category-specific predictive contributions. These findings indicate that consumption-side mitigation should adopt differentiated measures tailored to specific consumption categories and transmission pathways.
