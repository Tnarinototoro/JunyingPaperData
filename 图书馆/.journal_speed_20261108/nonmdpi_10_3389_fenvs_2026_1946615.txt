HKU Business School, the University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong SAR, China See more Article metrics View details Abstract Global climate governance increasingly relies on understanding the spatial mismatch between carbon emissions and ecosystem carbon sequestration capacity. As a critical regulating ecosystem service, carbon sequestration service flows exert substantial interregional spillover effects through spatial ecological interactions. However, existing research has largely focused on static supply–demand evaluations at regional scales, while the spatial flow processes and driving mechanisms of carbon sequestration service flows across cities remain insufficiently explored. This study quantified the supply, demand, and flows of carbon sequestration service flows across 333 prefecture-level administrative units in China from 2010 to 2024 using breakpoint–field strength models and spatial flow analysis. Furthermore, the XGBoost-SHAP model was adopted to detect the nonlinear driving mechanisms behind carbon sequestration service flows. The results showed that China’s carbon sequestration service flows presented a potential west-to-east linkage pattern, indicating a pronounced spatial mismatch between carbon sequestration supply areas and carbon emission demand areas. High-supply regions were predominantly distributed in western and northern China, whereas demand regions were concentrated in eastern coastal urban agglomerations. The total volume of carbon sequestration service flows first rose and then declined over the study period, while the radiation distance and spatial interaction of service flows continuously increased. The potential spatial linkage of carbon sequestration service flows were identified: Inner Mongolia-Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, Yunnan–Guizhou–Pearl River Delta, and Northeast China/Sichuan-Xinjiang-eastern coastal urban agglomerations. XGBoost-SHAP analysis showed that PRE, DEM, TEMP, and GDP exhibited prominent main effects. PRE and TEMP had the strongest interaction, with DEM-TEMP and DEM-GDP showing relatively strong interactions compared with other variable pairs. The findings revealed the potential spatial linkage pattern of carbon sequestration service flows and offered scientific support for interregional ecological compensation and spatially coordinated carbon governance in China. 1 Introduction Global climate change and carbon mitigation have become central issues in ecological governance and regional sustainable development in recent years. As an important regulating ecosystem service, carbon sequestration services absorb and store atmospheric carbon dioxide through vegetation photosynthesis, thereby mitigating the greenhouse effect and maintaining ecological security. However, with rapid industrialization and urbanization, carbon emission activities have increasingly concentrated in densely populated, industrially agglomerated, and economically developed regions, whereas ecological carbon sink resources are mainly distributed in forests, grasslands, wetlands, and ecologically functional areas with relatively lower levels of economic development. This spatial separation between carbon emission demand and carbon sequestration supply has resulted in significant spatial mismatch among regions. Consequently, carbon sequestration services are no longer merely localized ecological processes, but generate interregional connections via ecological spillover and spatial interactions, thereby forming interregional ecosystem service flows. Accordingly, this study adopts a normative spatial allocation assumption for carbon sequestration services and regards spatial ecological association as a theoretical medium for the spatial spillover of carbon sequestration services, thereby explaining the potential carbon sequestration service flows formed through ecosystem-service spillovers and interregional linkages. Based on the breakpoint–field strength model, the supply–demand matching and spatial allocation of carbon sequestration services are modeled according to the principle that the benefit effect of carbon sequestration services attenuates with increasing distance. It should be emphasized that the carbon sequestration service flows examined in this study essentially represent service linkages inferred from spatial relationships rather than physically observable trajectories of CO2 air-mass transport. Ecosystem service flow provides an important theoretical framework for understanding the cross-regional transfer of ecological benefits (De Groot et al., 2002) discussed the spatial externalities of ecosystem services from the perspective of ecosystem functions, goods, and services classification, laying the foundation for subsequent studies on ecosystem service valuation and spatial transfer (Serna-Chavez et al., 2014) developed a quantitative framework for assessing spatial ecosystem service flows and emphasized that ecosystem services should be understood through the spatial relationships among supply, connecting, and demand areas (Ma et al., 2017) systematically reviewed ecosystem service supply and demand studies and pointed out that ecosystem service research should move beyond static supply–demand assessment toward supply–demand matching, spatial flow, and regional coordination. In recent years, the potential carbon sequestration services flows has gradually been introduced into ecosystem service studies to explain the coupled relationships among ecological benefits, resource utilization, and human activities across distant regions, providing a new perspective for understanding interregional carbon sequestration flows, ecological compensation, and spatial governance. Existing studies on carbon sequestration services mainly focus on supply–demand assessment, ecosystem service flow simulation, and driving mechanism analysis. In terms of supply–demand assessment, previous studies have commonly used net primary productivity (NPP), net ecosystem productivity (NEP), carbon storage, and carbon emission data to estimate regional carbon sink capacity and carbon emission demand (Zhang et al., 2022) analyzed the impacts of climate change and land use change on ecosystem service interactions in the Xijiang River Basin and found that land use change significantly altered regional carbon sequestration capacity and ecosystem service synergies (Chen and Chi, 2022) revealed the spatial mismatch between ecosystem service supply and demand across China and found that economically developed regions often face higher ecosystem service demand pressures (Shao et al., 2024) assessed carbon emission and sequestration dynamics in the Three-North Shelterbelt Program area using the Google Earth Engine platform, providing technical support for large-scale carbon sink monitoring (Xu et al., 2026) analyzed the spatiotemporal variation and driving factors of carbon sinks in Xuzhou, a resource-depleted transition city, and demonstrated that land use structure and urban development processes significantly affect carbon sequestration capacity (Yu et al., 2023). Although these studies effectively revealed the spatial differences between carbon sequestration supply and demand, most focused primarily on supply–demand status assessments, while insufficient attention has been paid to how carbon sequestration services flow and link among regions. In terms of ecosystem service flow simulation, scholars have gradually shifted from static supply–demand pattern analysis to the characterization of service flow pathways, radiation ranges, and spatial transmission processes (Zhai et al., 2021) integrated ecosystem service supply, demand, and flow into an ecological compensation framework and explored ecological compensation optimization among cities in the Yellow River Basin (Du et al., 2023) investigated ecological compensation relationships in the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei region based on ecosystem service flows and argued that ecological compensation standards should consider the actual transfer processes of ecosystem services from supply areas to beneficiary areas (Yang et al., 2026) quantified the flow pathways of carbon sequestration services in Xinjiang and further optimized the spatial matching patterns between carbon sequestration supply and demand (Ma et al., 2025) studied ecological compensation optimization in the Yellow River Basin from the perspective of regulating service flows and emphasized that ecological compensation should not rely solely on administrative boundaries but should also incorporate ecosystem service transfer processes. These studies indicate that ecosystem service flows can more accurately reflect the potential spatial linkage of ecological benefits among regions. However, most existing studies focus on watersheds, ecological functional areas, or single regional scales, whereas systematic investigations of nationwide urban scales carbon sequestration service flow networks and their spatial radiation characteristics remain insufficient. Regarding methodological approaches for carbon sequestration service flow analysis, gravity models, circuit theory, breakpoint models, and field strength models have been widely used to characterize spatial linkages of ecosystem services (Fan et al., 2007) explored ecological asset transfer and valuation models, providing a foundation for studies on cross-regional ecological asset flows (Levy and Goldenberg, 2014) discussed the applicability of gravity models in social interaction studies, offering methodological references for describing distance-decay effects in ecosystem service flows (Simini et al., 2012) proposed a universal model for mobility and migration patterns, which also provides insights for ecosystem service flow simulation (Zhai et al., 2021) combined breakpoint models with ecological compensation studies to analyze the spatial transmission characteristics of carbon sequestration service flows. Existing studies suggest that breakpoint–field strength models can effectively describe the spatial distance-decay characteristics of ecosystem service flows; however, studies applying these models to carbon sequestration service flow simulation at the national urban scale remain limited. In terms of driving mechanism analysis, existing studies mainly focus on the impacts of natural environments, land use, population size, economic development, and energy consumption on carbon sink capacity and ecosystem service patterns (Wang et al., 2025) analyzed the evolutionary characteristics and driving factors of urban carbon emission performance in China and found that economic development level, industrial structure, and energy efficiency are key determinants of carbon emission performance (Liu et al., 2024) investigated the spatial association networks of carbon emissions based on carbon sink potential and found significant spatial network characteristics in regional carbon emission linkages (Huang et al., 2024) reviewed the carbon effects of land use systems and argued that land use change, industrial activities, and socioeconomic processes jointly shape regional carbon effects (Gai et al., 2025) analyzed changes and driving factors of ecosystem service supply and demand in the Yellow River Basin and demonstrated that natural conditions and human activities jointly influence ecosystem service supply–demand relationships. In recent years, machine learning methods have increasingly been applied to ecosystem service studies (Sun et al., 2025) employed machine learning methods to analyze the mechanisms underlying carbon sink changes in resource-depleted cities and revealed nonlinear influences of natural and socioeconomic factors. Compared with traditional linear models, the XGBoost-SHAP framework can quantify the driving factors of carbon sequestration service flows, decompose the marginal contribution of each variable, and capture the complex nonlinear relationships among variables (Wu et al., 2025). However, existing literature has paid limited attention to the synergistic effects of socioeconomic factors such as economic agglomeration, energy consumption and fiscal capacity on the spatial pattern of carbon sequestration service flows (Yuan et al., 2024). Existing studies have made substantial progress in carbon sequestration supply–demand assessment, ecosystem service flow simulation, driving mechanism identification, and ecological compensation research (He et al., 2023). Nevertheless, several limitations remain. First, most studies focus on watersheds, ecological functional areas, or urban agglomerations, while systematic investigations of nationwide urban carbon sequestration service flow networks are still lacking. Second, existing studies tend to focus more on static supply–demand matching, while neglecting the spatial transmission, radiation range, and interregional linkages of ecosystem service flows. Third, driving mechanism studies mainly focus on single factors or linear relationships, while nonlinear interactions between natural and socioeconomic factors remain insufficiently investigated. Finally, the relationships among carbon sequestration service flows, interregional ecological compensation, spatial governance, and spatial ecological interactions have not been adequately revealed. This study takes 333 prefecture-level administrative units in China as research units and integrates breakpoint and field strength models to simulate the spatial patterns, radiation distances, and supply–demand relationships of carbon sequestration service flows from 2010 to 2024. Furthermore, the XGBoost-SHAP models is employed to reveal the driving mechanisms of carbon sequestration service flows. Specifically, this study aims to: (1) identify the spatiotemporal evolution characteristics of carbon sequestration service supply and demand in Chinese cities; (2)characterize the spatial transmission pathways, radiation ranges, and interregional linkages of potential carbon sequestration service flows; (3) reveal the nonlinear driving mechanisms of natural and socioeconomic factors affecting carbon sequestration service flows; and (4) provide scientific support for interregional ecological compensation and spatially coordinated governance from the perspectives of ecosystem service flow and spatial interactions. Compared with previous studies, this study contributes to the literature in three aspects. First, it systematically characterizes the nationwide urban carbon sequestration service flow network in China, thereby extending the spatial scale of ecosystem service flow research. Second, it integrates breakpoint–field strength models and XGBoost-SHAP methods to reveal the spatial radiation characteristics and nonlinear driving mechanisms of carbon sequestration service flows. Third, it interprets carbon sequestration service flows within the frameworks of spatial mismatch, spatial interactions, and ecological compensation, providing new evidence for understanding ecological linkages and governance relationships between carbon sequestration supply areas and carbon emission demand areas. 2 Materials and methods 2.1 Study area China is located in eastern Asia along the western Pacific coast, extending from 73°33′E to 135°05′E and from 3°51′N to 53°33′N, with a total land area of approximately 9.6 million km2 (Figure 1). The country is characterized by diverse geomorphological conditions, including plains, plateaus, mountains, hills, and basins, which support abundant biological resources and multiple ecosystem types. Owing to its vast territorial extent, significant regional differences exist in ecological conditions, resource endowments, and socioeconomic development patterns. FIGURE 1 Study area. Based on natural resources, geological structure, and socioeconomic development conditions, mainland China can be divided into eight major economic regions, including the Northeast, Northern Coastal, Eastern Coastal, Southern Coastal, Middle Yellow River, Middle Yangtze River, Southwest, and Northwest regions (Gai et al., 2025). Considerable differences in land use structure, population distribution, industrial development, and economic growth among these regions have resulted in substantial spatial heterogeneity in carbon sequestration capacity and carbon emission intensity. Such regional disparities have further intensified the spatial mismatch between carbon sequestration supply and carbon emission demand, leading to interregional ecological spillover effects and ecosystem service flows. Therefore, incorporating carbon storage into a unified carbon emission allocation framework is essential for promoting regional carbon balance and ecological equity. Ecological compensation can alleviate the imbalance between ecological conservation and socioeconomic development among regions by coordinating stakeholder interests through economic instruments and enhancing incentives for environmental protection. This study takes municipal-level administrative units as the research scale. The study area covers 333 prefecture-level administrative units across mainland China, including 299 prefecture-level cities, 30 autonomous prefectures, and 4 municipalities directly under the central government, involving 31 provincial-level administrative regions (excluding Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macao due to data unavailability). Based on regional carbon storage and carbon emission data, the spatial distribution and flow intensity of carbon sequestration services were quantified. Furthermore, demographic and socioeconomic indicators were incorporated to analyze the driving mechanisms of carbon sequestration service flows in China. 2.2 Data source The datasets used in this study mainly included net primary productivity (NPP), carbon emissions, population density, administrative boundary data, and socioeconomic statistical data (Table 1). Among them, NPP data were used to characterize the supply capacity of carbon sequestration services, while carbon emission data were employed to represent carbon sequestration service demand. Population and socioeconomic data were further incorporated to analyze the driving mechanisms of carbon sequestration service flows. To ensure the consistency of spatial analysis, all raster datasets were preprocessed using ArcGIS 10.8 (Esri, Redlands, CA, USA) and resampled to a unified spatial resolution of 1,000 m × 1,000 m. All spatial datasets were projected into the Krasovsky-1940-Albers coordinate system to ensure spatial compatibility and analytical accuracy among different datasets. Spatially, the overall supply–demand agglomeration pattern of carbon sequestration service flows remained relatively stable from 2010 to 2024. High-supply areas were consistently concentrated in western Inner Mongolia, Tibet, Qinghai, Xinjiang, Northeast China, and Southwest China—regions characterized by abundant forest, grassland, and wetland resources, low levels of human disturbance, and stable carbon sequestration functions. During the same period, demand areas were generally clustered in manufacturing centers, densely populated regions, and urban agglomerations with high carbon emission intensity. In 2010, carbon sequestration demand areas were mainly scattered across core eastern coastal cities, including the Yangtze River Delta, Pearl River Delta, and Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei region, appearing as isolated and sporadically distributed patches. By 2015, the number of demand areas had increased considerably, extending to central and western provincial capitals such as Chengdu, Wuhan, Xi’an, and Zhengzhou, reflecting a trend of spatial expansion from the east toward the interior. In 2020, the distribution of demand areas became further densified; meanwhile, an increasing number of central and eastern cities emerged as demand areas, and coastal and riverine cities experienced a sharp rise in carbon sequestration demand, forming a contiguous belt along the eastern coast and the Yangtze River with an increasingly interconnected spatial network. By 2024, demand areas exhibited a pattern of higher density in the east and lower density in the west, with multiple concentrated clusters scattered across different regions. Medium-to high-value areas covered the broadest geographic range, while high-value areas formed contiguous and concentrated patches in the Yangtze River Delta, Pearl River Delta, Chengdu–Chongqing region, and the urban agglomerations along the middle reaches of the Yangtze River, presenting a multi-polar and relatively balanced spatial configuration. From 2010 to 2024, supply areas remained predominantly in the western region, while demand areas were consistently locked in eastern urban agglomerations, with no alleviation of geographic separation. The relationship evolved from a pattern of abundant supply and sparse demand in 2010 to one of interwoven supply and demand with multi-polar linkages by 2024. The XGBoost-SHAP framework was further applied to identify the driving mechanisms underlying carbon sequestration service flows. The main findings are summarized as follows. (1) The national total supply and demand of carbon sequestration services exhibited an overall trend of increasing first and then decreasing during the study period. (2) Carbon sequestration supply was predominantly distributed in western China, while carbon sequestration demand was persistently concentrated in eastern urban agglomerations. Carbon sequestration services generally flowed from ecologically functional western and northern regions with sparse populations and abundant forest resources to densely populated, economically developed, and high-carbon-emission eastern and southern regions, revealing evident spatial polarization and supply–demand mismatch in carbon sequestration service allocation. (3) The dominant spatial linkage patterns of potential carbon sequestration flows were identified, including ecological linkages originating in Inner Mongolia and benefiting the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei urban agglomeration, those originating in the Yunnan-Guizhou region and benefiting the Pearl River Delta urban agglomeration, and those originating in Northeast China, Sichuan, and Xinjiang and benefiting eastern coastal urban agglomerations. Overall, a distinct west-to-east linkage pattern of carbon sequestration was formed, representing a unidirectional cross-administrative linkage of ecological value from less developed regions to economically developed regions. (4) Climatic and topographic factors exerted stronger predictive power for carbon sequestration service flows than socioeconomic and anthropogenic factors. 5 Discussion The results reveal clear temporal variation and pronounced spatial differentiation in carbon sequestration service flows across Chinese cities. To further assess the robustness of the modeled flow patterns and interpret their spatial implications, this section discusses parameter uncertainty, the stability of the spatial distribution pattern, and the broader ecological and socioeconomic meaning of the observed service flows. Because the value of ranges from 0.4 to 0.8, some parameter uncertainty remains. In this study, = 0.6 was used as the benchmark value, with a perturbation range of ±0.2. Although the value of markedly affects the absolute magnitude of the estimated carbon sequestration service flows, it does not alter the overall spatial distribution pattern. This indicates that the spatial structure of the modeled flows is relatively robust to changes in , whereas the estimated absolute flow magnitude is more sensitive to parameter selection. Parameter primarily acts as a global scaling factor for the modeled carbon sequestration service flows. Variations in do not change the relative ranking of flow magnitudes across spatial units, thereby maintaining the stability of the overall spatial configuration. This finding indicates that the spatial patterns derived from the model provide a robust spatial reference for ecological applications, including ecological security pattern optimization, priority conservation area identification, and ecological functional zoning. Nevertheless, since Parameter substantially affects the absolute values of simulated service flows, its calibration should be rigorously justified when the model outputs are applied to monetary valuation practices, such as carbon sequestration economic accounting and the formulation of interregional ecological compensation standards. Apart from parametric uncertainty, the temporal dynamics of carbon sequestration supply and demand are associated with a combination of environmental and socioeconomic factors. Changes in land use, vegetation coverage, climatic conditions, and ecological restoration efforts substantially shape the spatiotemporal variations in carbon sequestration supply, while population agglomeration, economic development, and energy consumption largely shape the fluctuations in carbon sequestration demand. Large-scale ecological restoration projects and carbon governance policies further constitute critical institutional backgrounds for such spatiotemporal changes. Nevertheless, this study does not perform independent causal attribution or quantitative policy effect analysis. Accordingly, the aforementioned influencing factors are merely plausible explanatory mechanisms rather than empirically verified action pathways. At the spatial level, the modeled carbon sequestration service flows exhibit a clear potential west-to-east linkage pattern. From the observed spatial distribution, western China is characterized by relatively favorable ecological background conditions, abundant land resources, lower population density, and comparatively limited development intensity, which may provide an important basis for carbon sequestration service supply. In contrast, eastern coastal urban agglomerations are characterized by the continued concentration of population, capital, industrial activities, and energy consumption, and these socioeconomic characteristics are associated with stronger demand for carbon sequestration services. Consequently, ecological supply and socioeconomic demand exhibit a pronounced spatial mismatch across China’s urban systems. The XGBoost-SHAP results further demonstrated that climatic, topographic, vegetation-related, and socioeconomic variables were collectively associated with the spatial variation in carbon sequestration service flows. Among the eight explanatory variables, PRE exhibited the highest predictive importance, followed by DEM, TEMP, GDP, NDVI, NTL, POP, and LUI. The dominant predictive power of PRE, DEM, and TEMP highlighted that climatic and topographic conditions served as the fundamental associated factors underlying the spatial heterogeneity of carbon sequestration service flows. Meanwhile, the considerable contribution of GDP indicated that socioeconomic conditions also substantially modulate the spatial patterns of ecological service flows. SHAP interaction analysis further revealed the strongest pairwise interaction between PRE and TEMP, implying their combined associations with the spatial variability of carbon sequestration services. Collectively, these findings suggested that the observed spatial distribution of carbon sequestration service flows was associated with the combined effects of natural ecological backgrounds and anthropogenic socioeconomic demands. Importantly, SHAP values quantify variable contributions to model predictions rather than independent causal effects. Certain socioeconomic predictors may share inherent statistical correlations with carbon emission and demographic indicators that participate in the quantification of carbon sequestration service flows. Accordingly, the detected variable associations cannot be strictly interpreted as causal pathways. The specific mechanistic processes through which natural and socioeconomic factors regulate carbon sequestration service flows remain to be clarified in future research. Taken together, the national spatial pattern of carbon sequestration service flows indicates a latent ecological value linkage originating in supply-dominant western regions and benefiting demand-intensive central and eastern regions of China. This spatial configuration is not merely an ecological characteristic, but also reflects substantial regional disparities in population distribution, economic output, energy consumption, and anthropogenic development intensity. The concentrated socioeconomic activities within eastern urban agglomerations generate intensive demand for carbon sequestration services, whereas the favorable ecological endowments of western China underpin the stable provision of such ecological services. This distinct spatial separation between supply and demand underscores the necessity of integrating interregional ecological linkages into the formulation of carbon governance frameworks and ecological compensation policies. Accordingly, constructing a systematic interregional carbon governance mechanism based on horizontal ecological compensation is essential to balance regional equity and ecological sustainability. Such a mechanism can provide stable financial support for ecological conservation and restoration in key carbon supply regions while urging economically developed demand areas to undertake corresponding ecological costs and responsibilities. Interregional ecological compensation can effectively strengthen regional ecological protection incentives, improve the matching efficiency between ecological supply and socioeconomic demand, and promote balanced and sustainable regional development across the country. Nevertheless, the carbon sequestration service flows estimated in this study correspond to model-simulated spatial relationships rather than physically observed carbon transfer processes. Therefore, practical applications, including monetary valuation and ecological compensation standard formulation, require further empirical calibration and localized optimization to guarantee scientific rationality and practical operability.
