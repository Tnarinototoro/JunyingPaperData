Abstract
Climate change challenges the conservation of island-endemic plants by altering environmental suitability within geographically restricted landscapes. In the Balearic Islands, ecological research has informed the management of endemic flora, and modelling studies offer complementary information about present and future environmental suitability. We discuss selected evidence and argue for integrating ecological niche models into conservation planning for plants restricted to the Balearic Islands or shared exclusively with other islands. Existing projections indicate potentially substantial suitability losses in the systems studied, although limited taxonomic coverage prevents generalisation across the endemic flora. We highlight illustrative taxa and discuss how niche assessments could guide population reinforcement and identify translocation sites expected to remain suitable under future climates. Their application requires attention to sparse occurrence data, microclimatic variation, biotic interactions and uncertainty in climatic projections. We distinguish conservation from research priority and method choice, linking studies to uncertainty about site conditions that could change a management decision. Research should proceed alongside essential interventions, supported by sustained collaboration between scientists and conservation practitioners. Integrating niche projections with field evidence and species recovery planning could support more anticipatory conservation of Balearic endemic plants under climate change.
