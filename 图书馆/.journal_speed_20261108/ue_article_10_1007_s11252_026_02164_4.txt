However, sustaining these benefits under increasing water scarcity and heat exposure remains challenging. Although experimental research on VGR plant and substrate responses to water limitation is expanding, variation in water-supply regimes, experimental scale, duration, and outcome measures may limit the comparability and generalisability of existing evidence, underscoring the need for a structured assessment of its scope and limitations. Accordingly, we combined a bibliometric analysis of 943 Scopus-indexed articles (2005–2025) with a structured synthesis of 161 empirical studies to map evidence across water-supply regimes, experimental settings, heat-exposure designs, and ecosystem services. We found uneven geographical coverage, with research concentrated in a few cities and established institutional sites, several of which supported long-term research and multiple publications. Although 68 studies examined heat exposure alongside water limitation, two imposed warming under water-limited conditions, and interpretation was constrained by limitations in treatment comparisons, measurement timing, sensor reliability, correlated predictor variables, and changes in irrigation practices. We identified major evidence gaps concerning links between plant responses and the maintenance or recovery of VGR ecosystem services, across cooling, habitat and biodiversity, stormwater regulation, and carbon regulation. Key priorities to address these gaps are proposed, centred on linking plant responses to service outcomes over the same drying and recovery periods, improving comparability across studies, and broadening the roof environments represented. These priorities may guide future work on VGR design and management under water-limited conditions. Highlights Thematic analysis of 943 articles and empirical evidence mapping of 161 studies revealed differences between thematic emphasis and empirical evidence coverage. Evidence linking plant responses to the maintenance and recovery of VGR ecosystem-service provision remains limited. Only two included studies imposed warming under water-limited conditions; evidence on interactions between warming and water-limited conditions remained limited. Research coverage was uneven across regions, with studies also concentrated in a few cities and experimental sites.
