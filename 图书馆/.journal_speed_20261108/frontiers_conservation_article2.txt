Abstract
The Apennine brown bear (Ursus arctos marsicanus) is a critically endangered and genetically depauperate bear population, endemic to central Italy. Long-term conservation of this unique subspecies requires reliable genetic tools to monitor population dynamics and genetic variability, particularly from non-invasively collected samples. In this study, an ad hoc panel of 89 highly informative single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) was developed from whole-genome data and genotyped using a targeted next-generation sequencing approach based on Molecular Inversion Probes (MIPs). This is a provisional file, not the final typeset article The panel was applied to genotype invasive (blood and tissue) and non-invasive (hair) samples from 26 Apennine bears, achieving complete genotyping success and very low sub-MIP discordant calls, even in degraded hair-derived DNA. Despite the low genetic variability and high relatedness characterizing the population, the selected SNPs unambiguously discriminated all individuals, captured patterns of residual genetic variation, and confirmed parent–offspring relationships previously inferred from field observations. These results show that combining population-specific SNP markers with MIP technology can provide accurate, reproducible genotypes even from low-quality non-invasive samples. The proposed approach represents a starting point for implementing future genetic monitoring programs and offers a valuable tool for the long-term assessment of demographic and genetic trends in the Apennine brown bear, with potential methodological applications to other small and genetically depauperate animal populations.
