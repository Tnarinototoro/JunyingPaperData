This study assesses the current and future role of non-conventional water resources (NCW) (reclaimed and desalinated water), in compensating for declining conventional supplies in southeastern Spain. Using participatory Fuzzy Cognitive Mapping (FCM), we analysed four scenarios: expansion of NCW, desalination cost recovery, investment in renewable energy and capacity building. The model identified 25 components and 37 causal relationships, highlighting the importance of economic, institutional, environmental, social and technical drivers. Results show that NCW expansion and renewable energy investment generate the greatest improvements in water supply guarantee and agricultural income, while capacity building has moderate positive effects. In contrast, cost recovery reduces desalinated water use and agricultural income. Implementation of the Segura River Basin Management Plan (SRBMP) further increases the strategic relevance of NCW, underscoring their key role in integrated water planning in semi-arid regions.
