Cancel × FirstView articles Contents Report Adaptive management under uncertainty: sustained participatory management of endemic species in Brazilian Cerrado Maria Auxiliadora Drumond , Lorena Cristina Lana Pinto , Júlia de Matos Nogueira , Adriana Assunção de Carvalho , Raquel Hosken Pereira da Silva , Irla Paula Stopa Rodrigues , Livia Comini Published online by Cambridge University Press: 03 November 2026 , pp. 1-6 Article Get access Check if you have access via personal or institutional login Log in Register Export citation View abstract Given land-use change threatening both biodiversity and traditional extractive livelihoods since 2004, an action research process in Minas Gerais (Brazil) has focused on the management of two endemic species: the giant earthworm Rhinodrilus alatus and the pequi tree Caryocar brasiliense . Socio-ecological assessments, ecological field surveys and market analyses generated the evidence that extractivists and public institutions used to negotiate management agreements. Despite more than a decade of institutional uncertainty and the absence of formal regulation, local actors maintained voluntary harvesting restrictions, including a closed season during the reproductive period. Ecological evidence supported reassessing the conservation status of R. alatus , enabling discussions on regulated sustainable use. To address seasonal income losses, we developed a community-based pequi processing initiative that aligns livelihood activities with ecological cycles. Over two decades, sustained collaboration, co-produced knowledge and livelihood diversification reduced conflict, strengthened local organization and supported adaptive management under legal uncertainty. This demonstrates that long-term participatory processes can sustain conservation outcomes and reconcile biodiversity conservation with rural livelihoods in socially and biologically diverse landscapes. Research Paper Conservation areas in the eastern Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt: a dynamic conceptual framework under land-use change Oscar Campos , Tania Escalante , Alma Mendoza-Ponce Published online by Cambridge University Press: 30 October 2026 , pp. 1-10 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract The Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt is a biogeographical province, and its eastern sub-province faces intense pressures from land-use and land-cover change (LULCC). This study integrates a systematic conservation planning approach, species distribution models and LULCC projections to identify areas that facilitate the conservation of endemic biodiversity under a dynamic conceptual framework. We selected 13 species of amphibians and mammals as surrogates, obtained their geographical distributions and analysed the distributional patterns of species richness and rarity, which showed significant concentrations in volcanic mountain systems. Historical and future LULCC models indicated a continued expansion of rainfed agriculture, potentially reaching 70% of the study area by 2050, alongside a severe reduction in temperate forest. Currently, 65% of the potential habitat of endemic species and 43% of the potential habitat of geographically rare species are affected. Under pessimistic future scenarios, losses could exceed 95%. Although the priority conservation areas identified are relatively continuous core areas at present, future projections showed considerable fragmentation. We propose a possible dynamic conservation network linking permanent nodes with dynamic nodes and temporary ecological corridors according to ecological, seasonal or anthropogenic pressures. Furthermore, this approach integrates ecological, socioeconomic and governance dimensions to reconcile conservation with sustainable practices in highly transformed landscapes. A framework for maximizing conservation stewardship effectiveness in mosaic landscapes: bringing together ecological priority and social feasibility Vladimir V Kricsfalusy , Maureen G Reed , Marissa Glofcheskie , Kakon Chakma , John Kindrachuk Published online by Cambridge University Press: 29 October 2026 , pp. 1-11 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract Effective conservation of rare species requires approaches that extend beyond protected areas and integrate the perspectives of diverse landowners. We synthesized results from rare plant assessments with landowner perceptions and conservation values in landscapes with a mix of ownership and management types to produce an integrated framework for maximizing stewardship effectiveness. We combined field surveys of rare plant species across five landownership types (private, provincial, federal, non-profit and leaseholder) with semi-structured interviews of representatives of these groups in the Redberry Lake Biosphere Region of Canada to identify conservation values, landowner attitudes, perceived challenges, preferred incentives and legislative awareness across ownership types. Derived from the results, our framework drew attention to ecological priority and social feasibility as key elements of integrated conservation management. The framework provided operational definitions for each element and highlighted that even regional conservation strategies must account for variation in landownership and values. This framework therefore serves as a cohesive approach to applying both ecological and social evidence to inform decision-making for biodiversity conservation in mosaic landscapes. Our experience suggests that bringing together evidence from the natural and social sciences offers novel insights, and the framework we propose may be transferable across a range of conservation contexts. Report Investigating the spatial distribution of a marine top predator under elevated ambient sound conditions Amber D Fandel , Kristin Hodge , Aaron Rice , Helen Bailey Published online by Cambridge University Press: 28 October 2026 , pp. 1-6 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract Many protected marine mammals use sound as a primary sensory modality for communication and foraging, so acoustic disruptions can be costly. To investigate the behavioural responses of a marine top predator, the common bottlenose dolphin ( Tursiops truncatus ), to underwater sound levels, we used a non-invasive method of localizing dolphin pods using their whistles detected on a fixed array. Additionally, for the first time, we tracked them over time, accounting for acoustic localization error using a state–space movement model. We investigated movement parameters (speed and path straightness) and the animals’ spatial distribution in relation to broadband (10–7080 Hz) and mid-frequency (2820–7080 Hz) ambient sound levels. Dolphins utilized different areas under elevated broadband and mid-frequency ambient sound levels, but the novel analysis of travel speed and directness of dolphin pod movement did not show significant variations. Bottlenose dolphins in the Mid-Atlantic Bight probably were not habituated to elevated ambient sound levels, as evidenced by their altered habitat use. Altered distributions in response to changes in the acoustic environment could impact the foraging success, health and vital rates of this protected species. Comment Forecastable Sargassum blooms are not automatic carbon removal: a policy triage framework for habitat, coastal risk and biomass use Juan J Segura Published online by Cambridge University Press: 27 October 2026 , pp. 1-3 Article You have access Access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract Recent work suggests that the Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt is not only a recurring coastal nuisance but a partly predictable offshore biomass system. This Comment argues that such predictability should be used for policy triage, not for automatic carbon removal claims. Sargassum may be an offshore habitat, a nearshore hazard, wet waste, feedstock or possible carbon management material depending on its location, timing and final fate. Forecasts can therefore support practical decisions about whether to leave biomass offshore, harvest it selectively, convert it under verified conditions or dispose of it safely. The proposed framework clarifies the minimum evidence needed before Sargassum management is described as blue carbon or marine carbon dioxide removal. From red lights to ‘greener’ driving? The potential of mobile traffic signal countdown information for urban emission mitigation Song Zhou , Xiang Jia , Yunpeng Zhou , Pei Lei Published online by Cambridge University Press: 15 October 2026 , pp. 1-4 Article You have access Access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract Urban transport remains an important source of energy consumption and air pollutant emissions, particularly under the transient driving conditions associated with signalized intersections. This Comment examines the integration of real-time traffic signal countdown information into mobile navigation platforms as a potential low-cost digital behavioural intervention for improving urban transport efficiency. Drawing on published field observations and simulation studies, we show that previous research has associated access to signal timing information with smoother driving trajectories, reduced unnecessary idling and acceleration and lower fuel consumption and traffic-related emissions under specific traffic and control conditions. Building on this evidence, our aim is to place countdown-informed driving within a broader environmental management framework and to examine how existing digital infrastructure could be used to support behaviour-based emission mitigation without requiring major physical infrastructure changes. We further identify driver response, safety, signal data reliability, accessibility and governance as critical factors determining whether these potential benefits can be realized in practice. More broadly, we propose traffic signal countdown information as an illustrative case of how digitally enabled behavioural adaptation may complement technological and infrastructural strategies for more sustainable urban mobility. Corrigendum An investigative study of non-documentary television footage as a data source for community composition and biodiversity research – CORRIGENDUM Christopher Crowder , Thabisa McAnyana , Geoffrey S Cook Published online by Cambridge University Press: 13 October 2026 , p. 1 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation Research Paper Change in the use and cover of the Amazonian savanna enclaves jeopardizes non-volant small mammals (Mammalia: Didelphimorphia, Rodentia) Simone Almeida Pena , Cláudia Regina Silva , Rafaela Jemely Rodrigues Alexandre , Letícia Lima Correia , Felipe Baia Rodrigues , Thiago Bernardi Vieira Published online by Cambridge University Press: 23 September 2026 , pp. 1-9 Article Get access Check if you have access via personal or institutional login Log in Register Export citation View abstract The Brazilian Amazon’s savanna enclaves harbour unique biodiversity but face escalating degradation, and their conservation value for non-volant small mammals (orders Didelphimorphia and Rodentia) remains poorly quantified. Using species distribution models and spatial prioritization ( Zonation software), we assessed: (1) whether protected areas (PAs) and Indigenous territories (ITs) overlap with high-importance habitats; (2) the mapping of priority savanna enclaves to support the conservation of species found exclusively in these habitats; and (3) the correlation between the remaining natural vegetation cover and conservation priority. Our results reveal that while ITs and strictly protected areas (SPAs) effectively conserve some functional guilds (e.g., insectivorous marsupials), critical savanna enclaves are both biologically significant and highly degraded. Negative correlations between habitat importance and vegetation cover highlight urgent conservation gaps. We advocate for expanded PA networks targeting savanna enclaves, habitat restoration and stronger integration of IT management to safeguard these irreplaceable ecosystems and their exclusive fauna. Security evaluation and coupling coordination of the water–energy–food–carbon system in Northeast China Yanqiu Wang , Shumin Liu , Lianwei Liu , Anni Zhang , Yutianyi Zhang Published online by Cambridge University Press: 11 September 2026 , pp. 1-8 Article Get access Check if you have access via personal or institutional login Log in Register Export citation View abstract In China’s push towards peak carbon and carbon neutrality targets, the coordinated, secure and sustainable development of water resources, energy, food and carbon is of vital importance. To analyse the current security status and degree of coupling coordination in the water–energy–food–carbon (WEFC) coupling system in Northeast China, this paper constructs an indicator system to evaluate the security level and changes over 2013–2022 of the system by integrating an entropy approach, a coupling coordination mechanism and a comprehensive safety evaluation model. The coupling coordination degree of the WEFC system is also predicted for the period 2023–2030 using a long short-term memory model. The analysis demonstrates that the comprehensive security status of the WEFC system in Northeast China remained relatively low from 2013 to 2022, with the subsystem of water resources exerting the most pronounced influence. The security evaluation indices of the energy and carbon subsystems showed steady growth, while those of the water resources and food subsystems experienced fluctuating growth. The coupling coordination degree underwent minor fluctuations due to policy interventions and climatic impacts but revealed a gradually improving trajectory. Influenced by resource endowments, the northern and southern regions of Northeast China displayed more disharmony than the central region (Jilin > Liaoning > Heilongjiang) in terms of coordination levels. The coupling coordination degree is expected to continue to increase and progress towards a high-level coordination state by 2030. Corrigendum Both restored forest and birdsong promote psychological well-being – CORRIGENDUM Nils Peterson , Lincoln R Larson , Trenton Ford , Payton DeMay , Faith Swartz , JD Kluttz , Kushagra Meshram , Caitlin McDade , Hannah Desrochers , Aaron Hipp , Jackson Webb , Made Ayu Pratiwi , Erin Foy , Christian Jake Bourke Published online by Cambridge University Press: 03 August 2026 , p. 1 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation Research Paper Both restored forest and birdsong promote psychological well-being Nils Peterson , Lincoln R Larson , Trenton Ford , Payton DeMay , Faith Swartz , JD Kluttz , Kushagra Meshram , Caitlin McDade , Hannah Desrochers , Aaron Hipp , Jackson Webb , Made Ayu Pratiwi , Erin Foy , Christian Jake Bourke Published online by Cambridge University Press: 17 July 2026 , pp. 1-9 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract We contribute to research addressing links between forest restoration and human well-being with an experimental study testing how exposure to a restored forest and birdsong impact mental stress, positive and negative experiences and perceived restorativeness. This study engaged 106 campus participants at North Carolina State University in a 2 × 2 experiment to assess the impacts of soundscapes (i.e., traffic noise versus birdsong) and a restored riparian forest (compared to proximate grey space). Soundscape and location were drivers of all well-being metrics (p < 0.001 for main effects in every case, with effect sizes ranging from η 2 = 0.05 to 0.34). The restored forest with birdsong combination elicited the lowest stress, lowest negative experiences, highest positive experiences and highest perceived restorativeness (p < 0.05). The grey space with traffic noise combination was always the least beneficial (p < 0.05), and mixed treatments yielded intermediate results. These results build evidence that exposure to restored forest reduces stress, increases well-being and assists with perceived restorativeness; they also suggest that birdsong soundscapes have additive beneficial effects. These findings support the value of ecological restoration in urban areas, particularly when landscape restoration is accompanied by restoration of bird communities. An investigative study of non-documentary television footage as a data source for community composition and biodiversity research Christopher Crowder , Thabisa McAnyana , Geoffrey S Cook Published online by Cambridge University Press: 13 July 2026 , pp. 1-9 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract Technology has created opportunities to explore non-traditional data sources for investigating patterns in community composition and biodiversity over time. Television shows set in natural environments implicitly contain ecological information. Through an investigative lens, we explored the potential utility of this data source for describing community composition and diversity trends given uncertainties in footage collection. We reviewed 14 seasons of the TV series Survivor (2016–2023) to quantify animal frequency and total appearances, describing the animals observed and calculating common diversity metrics. A total of 40 301 individuals representing 182 taxa were identified, with species accumulation analysis estimating up to 198 identifiable taxa detectable from Survivor footage. The number of animals observed was inherently biased towards more charismatic fauna, while the proportion of screen time was predominately given over to invertebrates. Temporal analyses suggested slight declines in biodiversity and species richness. However, we note several limitations that constrain the use of these data, particularly the unknown amount of sampling effort, as well as the absence of temporal and spatial information necessary for more robust analyses. Although we are currently sceptical of its application in ecological and conservation research, this data source may have greater value if paired with additional contextual information and metadata. Multi-species approaches are necessary to optimize conservation: integrating a genetic algorithm with stochastic models in the Murray–Darling Basin (Australia) Rupert Mathwin , Aaron Zecchin , Skye Wassens , Matthew S Gibbs , Kate Mason , Corey J.A. Bradshaw Published online by Cambridge University Press: 08 July 2026 , pp. 1-9 Article You have access Access Open access PDF HTML Export citation View abstract Environmental water delivery is implemented globally to redress the ecological impacts of river regulation and consumptive water use. Allocation must balance the competing (and often conflicting) needs of humans, a diversity of water-dependent species and abiotic processes in the managed system. To optimize water allocation for conservation we developed a novel optimization framework that integrated a genetic algorithm with an integrated systems model to refine near-optimal allocations that maximize outcomes for a shrub ( Duma florulenta ), tree ( Eucalyptus camaldulensis ) and tree frog ( Ranoidea raniformis ) in one of the world’s most regulated catchments: the Murray–Darling Basin (Australia). Our algorithm weighted 16 wetland attributes and optimized those weights to prioritize allocation over a 25-year forecast window. The best-performing solutions required no additional resources but improved outcomes for the two priority plants and doubled the surviving frog populations. Modifying the multi-user model to only consider R. raniformis improved outcomes for that species at the expense of the two plant species, highlighting the risks of allocating a shared resource through a single-species lens. We demonstrate the value of optimization and multi-species approaches as decision-support tools for environmental water allocation and similar resource allocation problems in conservation. Online plant trade in India: species inventory and invasion risk assessment Kesang Bhutia , NA Aravind Published online by Cambridge University Press: 07 July 2026 , pp. 1-8 Article Get access Check if you have access via personal or institutional login Log in Register Export citation View abstract The ornamental horticulture industry is a major pathway for the introduction of invasive alien species (IAS), with online trade accelerating their spread by expanding market access. This study compiled an inventory of plant species in India’s online market, assessed native, alien and invasive plants and evaluated invasion risk using global naturalized and invasive records. Data from three leading online plant retailers revealed 1856 species spanning 1045 genera and 194 families. Alien species (1371) outnumbered native species (480), with Apocynaceae and Cactaceae being the most species-rich families. Herbs were the predominant life form, and most alien taxa originated from the Americas. Cross-referencing with global databases identified 327 invasive and 639 naturalized alien species among those traded online. Thirty-nine species that are invasive elsewhere are already naturalized in India (high risk), 239 are invasive elsewhere but not yet naturalized locally (moderate risk) and 2 are low risk. Additionally, 57 species already invasive in India are sold online. These findings indicate a substantial biological invasions risk via online ornamental plant trade in India, highlighting the need for stronger regulations, risk-assessment protocols, enhanced awareness and coordinated action among the ornamental plant industry, government agencies and conservation organizations to curb the spread of IAS.
