Summary
The Trans-Mexican V01canic Belt is a biogeographical province ， and its eastern sub-province
faces intense pressures from land-use and land-cover change (LULCC) ． This study integrates a
systematic conservation planning approach, species distribution models and LULCC projec-
tions tO identify areas that facilitate the conservation Of endemic biodiversity under a dynamic
conceptual framework. We selected 13 species 0f amphibians and mammals as surrogates,
obtained their geographical distributions and analysed the distributional patterns 0f species
richness and rarlty, WhiCh showed significant concentrations in volcanic mountam systems.
Historical and future LULCC models indicated a continued expansion 0f rainfed agriculture,
potentially reaching 70 ％ of the study area by 2050 ， alongside a severe reduction in temperate
forest. CurrentIy, 65 ％ of the potential habitat of endemic species and 43 ％ of the potential
habitat of geographically rare species are affected. Under pessimistic future scenarios, losses
could exceed 95 ％ ， Although the priority conservation areas identified are relatively continuous
core areas at present, future projecUons showed considerable fragmentation. We propose a
possible dynamic conservation network linking permanent nodes with dynamic nodes and
temporary ecological corridors according tO ecological seasonal or anthropogenic pressures.
Furthermore, thiS approach integrates ecological, socioeconomic and governance dimensions
tO reconcile conservation with sustainable practices in highly transformed landscapes.

Threats tO biodiversity can be classified as ultimate or proximate. Ultimate threats include
factors such as population growth and the expansion 0f global markets, the social, economic and
political drivers of which are often beyond the direct control of conservation planners (Lambin
et al. 200D ． Proximate threats are defined as localized expressions 0f ultimate threats that
directly affect biodiversity at regional or local scales (Pressey et al. 2007 ） ． Proximate threats are
aISO dynamic, as can increase or decrease in extent intensity, operating as space—time
mosaics 0f impact followed by recovery (Lambin et al. 200L Pressey et al. 2007 ） ．
One dynamic proxnnate threat is land-use and land-cover change (LULCC), in which
natural vegetation is transformed for alternative uses commonly related tO the agricultural
sector, introduced vegetation, forest plantations, deforestation and urban development
(Velåzquez et al. 2003 ， SuIIivan et al. 2018 ） ， Moreover ， conservation areas are open systems that
could be affected by LULCC both within and outside their boundaries (Janzen 1983 ， Bengtsson
et al. 2003 ， Figueroa & Sånchez-Cordero 2008 ， Figueroa et al. 201L Aguilar Tomasini et al.
2020 ） ． Currently in Mexico, there is no effective strategy t0 address dynamic threats such as
LULCC. ln particular, protected areas are not effective at stopping LULCC (Aguilar-Tomasini
et al. 2020 ） in the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt (TMVB) biogeographical province (Morrone
2014 ， Morrone et al. 2017 ） in CentraI Mexico. As a consequence, LULCC models have been built
t0 address this issue (Mendoza- Ponce et al. 2018 ， 2020 ） ．
A dynamic conceptual framework may be incorporated intO systematic conservation
planning (SCP) through LULCC models. SCP is a structured, stepwise protocol for prioritizing
conservation area networks that also use surrogates as a representation Of the whole biodiversity
0f an area (Margules & Sarkar 2007 ） ． The selection 0f such surrogates is a critical component in
conservation prioritization (Margules & Sarkar 2007 ） ， and the process includes feedback,
revision and reiteration 0f every step at all stages 0f the process (Margules & Pressey 2000 ，
Margules & Sarkar 2007 ） ， SCP also recognizes that tangible results and costs can be associated
with particular actions, such as prioritizing certain land-use practices outside protected areas,
which could be an efficient complementary strategy t0 reduce biodiversity loss (TaIIis et al. 2021,
