Selvam * Department of Commerce, School of Social Sciences and Languages, Vellore Institute of Technology, Vellore, Tamil Nadu, India Article metrics View details Abstract Introduction: The rapid growth of electric vehicles (EVs) registrations requires a better understanding of how EV registrations vary across geographical areas. This study examines the geographical and temporal patterns of EV registrations across Regional Transport Office (RTO) regions in Tamil Nadu from 2022 to 2025. Methods: Secondary data were obtained from the official Tamil Nadu EV Registration portal. Registrations of electric two-wheelers, three-wheelers, and four-wheelers were analyzed using temporal trends, vehicle-category composition, RTO-level growth, registration concentration, and a separate analysis of Chennai RTO regions. Results: Total EV registrations increased from 69,785 in 2022 to 165,818 in 2025, representing a 137.61% increase. Two-wheelers remained the dominant category, while four-wheelers recorded substantial growth. Tiruppur and Coimbatore emerged as important RTOs in 2025. The share of the top five and top ten RTOs decreased from 14.24% and 24.52% in 2022 to 11.28% and 19.90% in 2025, respectively. Chennai registrations increased from 11,663 to 17,499 during the study period. Discussion: The findings show a clear increase in EV registrations across Tamil Nadu, along with changes in the contribution of different RTO areas and vehicle categories. The lower shares of the top five and top ten RTO areas in 2025 compared with 2022 suggest that EV registrations were spread across a wider range of RTO areas. However, registration data only show the number of vehicles registered and do not provide information about actual vehicle use or the charging infrastructure required. 1 Introduction Transportation is an important aspect of sustainable urban development because it has a direct impact on emissions, air quality, land and energy use, and urban development. Therefore, achieving sustainable mobility requires improvements in transportation technologies and supporting infrastructure (Banister, 2008; Creutzig et al., 2015). In this transition, Electric Vehicles (EVs) have become an important way to reduce dependence on petroleum-based fuels and promote cleaner transportation (Hawkins et al., 2013; Sierzchula et al., 2014; International Energy Agency, 2025). The adoption of electric vehicles has increased rapidly worldwide over the past decade. The growth of EVs has been supported by advances in battery technology, government initiatives, various model availability, and greater environmental awareness (Coffman et al., 2017; Rezvani et al., 2015; Sierzchula et al., 2014). According to International Energy Agency (2025), global electric-car sales exceeded 17 million in 2024, making up more than one-fifth of new car sales worldwide. This growth shows that EVs are becoming an important part of the shift to sustainable transportation. However, EV growth is not the same in all places. Countries, states, and cities differ in their economic conditions, transportation systems, government policies, electricity infrastructure and charging facilities. Previous studies have found that factors such as financial incentives, environmental concerns, income, socioeconomic conditions, and infrastructure availability influence EV adoption (Degirmenci and Breitner, 2017; Ghasri et al., 2019; Rezvani et al., 2015; Sierzchula et al., 2014). Therefore, national or state-level registration totals may not fully represent differences in EV registration activity across smaller geographical areas. Understanding the geographical distribution of EV registrations is therefore important for examining how electric mobility develops across different areas. EV registration levels may vary considerably beyond metropolitan areas, industrial centers, smaller towns and semi-urban regions. Such geographical variation can provide an indication of where EV registration activity is concentrated and whether registration growth is becoming more widely distributed over time. Analyzing the geographical distribution of EVs is important for transportation and urban planning. The growth of EV registrations may create different infrastructure considerations across locations. Areas with higher numbers of EV registrations may warrant further investigation of charging infrastructure requirements (Funke et al., 2019; Pagany et al., 2019). Studies show that charging stations should be located based on the number of EVs rather than providing same number of stations everywhere (He et al., 2015; Pagany et al., 2019). Recent research has further applied spatial planning, demand estimation, and location-allocation methods to identify suitable locations for charging infrastructure (He et al., 2022; Torkey and Abdelgawad, 2022; Zhang and Kontou, 2025). However, EV registration data alone do not indicate where vehicles are actually used, the distances traveled, charging behavior, or the amount and location of charging capacity required. Therefore, registration patterns should be interpreted as evidence of geographical registration activity rather than as direct evidence of charging-infrastructure requirements. Registration counts alone cannot determine the number, location, or capacity of charging facilities required. India is a suitable country for examining regional EV growth because its transportation system relies heavily on two-wheelers and three-wheelers for personal and commercial transportation. Consequently, these vehicle categories form an important part of India's EV market (Chhikara et al., 2021; Kumar and Alok, 2020; International Energy Agency, 2025). Although EV growth has been supported by national and state-level policies, challenges related to charging infrastructure, vehicle cost, technology, government policies, and manufacturing continue to influence EV adoption (Chhikara et al., 2021; Mishra et al., 2021; Tarei et al., 2021). Charging infrastructure also remains important as a consideration in India's EV development (Patel et al., 2024). However, state-level EV figures may not fully reflect differences between regions. Metropolitan cities, industrial centers, and smaller urban areas may experience different patterns of EV growth due to differences in economic activity, transportation needs, and infrastructure. Tamil Nadu provides a suitable setting for examining these differences, as it includes major urban and industrial centers such as Chennai, Coimbatore, Tiruppur, Hosur, and Erode. State-level totals can indicate whether EV registrations are increasing, but they cannot identify where this growth is occurring within the state. RTO-level registration data provide a more geographically detailed basis for examining differences in registration levels between administrative areas. This is particularly relevant for Tamil Nadu, where major metropolitan and industrial centers coexist with smaller urban and semi-urban areas. Previous studies have examined consumer attitudes, adoption barriers, policies, and charging infrastructure, relatively less attention has been given to the actual geographical distribution of EV registrations at the sub-state level in India. The present study focuses specifically on administrative EV registration records at the RTO level to describe how registration activity varies geographically and changes over time with Tamil Nadu. Registration data can provide direct evidence of where EVs are being registered and how their distribution changes over time. More specifically, there is scope to examine how EV registration levels have changed across RTO areas over multiple years and whether growth remains concentrated in major urban centers or becomes more evenly distributed across other parts of the state. Such analysis can describe differences in registration activity across RTO areas without assuming that registration counts represent individual-level adoption behavior or establish the causes of regional differences. The present study therefore examines the geographical and temporal patterns of EV registrations across Tamil Nadu using RTO-level data from 2022 to 2025. It analyzes total EV registrations, vehicle-category differences, regional variation, and growth patterns across RTO regions, with particular attention to Chennai. The study also compares Chennai's registration growth with the overall growth observed across Tamil Nadu. This comparison is important because Chennai represents a major metropolitan center, while other RTO areas may account for an increasing proportion of the state's EV registrations. Examining Chennai within the wider state context therefore provides an opportunity to assess whether the growth in EV registrations is increasingly distributed beyond the state's major metropolitan center. The study focuses on EV registration levels and their geographical distribution rather than treating registration counts as direct measures of individual-level EV adoption. It also does not attempt to establish causal relationships between regional EV growth and factors such as income, population, urbanization, policy incentives, economic activity, or charging infrastructure, because these explanatory variables are not directly measured in the present dataset. 1.1 The study addresses the following objectives: To examine the temporal evolution of EV registrations in Tamil Nadu between 2022 and 2025. To analyse changes in registrations of electric two-wheelers, three-wheelers, and four-wheelers. To examine geographical differences in EV registration levels across RTO regions. To examine the absolute and percentage growth in EV registrations between 2022 and 2025. To assess the geographical distribution and concentration of EV registrations across RTO areas in Tamil Nadu. To examine Chennai's EV registration growth in comparison with the overall pattern observed across Tamil Nadu. By examining RTO-level registration data over four consecutive years, this study provides a geographically detailed description of EV registration growth across Tamil Nadu. The analysis contributes to understanding how EV registration activity varies between metropolitan, industrial, and other RTO areas and whether the geographical concentration of registrations changes over time. The findings provide a baseline for future research examining the demographic, economic, infrastructural, and policy factors that may contribute to regional differences in EV development. 2 Literature review 2.1 Sustainable mobility and electric vehicles Sustainable mobility has become an important part of urban development because transportation systems contribute significantly to environmental and energy pressures. Sustainable mobility focuses on the need to reconsider transportation systems in relation to environmental sustainability and long-term urban development (Banister, 2008). Likewise, Creutzig et al. (2015) highlighted transportation as a major challenge for climate-change mitigation and highlighted the importance of technological and structural changes in transportation systems. Electric mobility plays an important role in the shift to EVs. EVs produce no direct tailpipe emissions during operation. However, their overall environmental performance also depends on the factors such as vehicle manufacturing and battery production (Hawkins et al., 2013). Therefore, transportation electrification is a part of broader change in transportation and energy systems. As EV markets expand, the transition also creates new requirements for supporting infrastructure. Greater numbers of EVs can also increase demand for home, workplace and public charging infrastructure, with requirements varying according to vehicle characteristics, charging behavior and geographical patterns of ownership (Funke et al., 2019). The literature on sustainable mobility shows that EV should not be viewed only as an individual means of transportation. Their growth is also related to the way transportation systems are organized and the infrastructure needed to support their use. This provide a basis for studying not only the overall growth of EVs but also how EV registrations are distributed across different geographical areas. 2.2 Global and indian electric vehicle adoption EV adoption is influenced by a combination of technological, economic, and behavioral factors (Sierzchula et al., 2014). Vehicle characteristics, charging availability and consumer perceptions have also been identified as important factors (Rezvani et al., 2015). Coffman et al. (2017) further emphasized the roles of economic, technological, policy and behavioral factors, in EV adoption. Consumer perceptions of environmental benefits, vehicle price and driving range can also affect willingness to adopt EVs (Degirmenci and Breitner, 2017). India's EV transition differs from that of developed automobile markets because two-wheelers and three-wheelers constitute an important part of daily and commercial transportation. Kumar and Alok (2020) highlighted the importance of India's unique market conditions, while Chhikara et al. (2021) identified consumers and infrastructure related factors influencing EV adoption. Limited infrastructure remains an important challenge to India's EV transition. Tarei et al. (2021) identified technological, infrastructural, financial, behavioral and external barriers associated with EV adoption in India. Mishra et al. (2021) emphasized the importance of expanding and improving charging infrastructure remain important for the development of the EV ecosystem. Expert-based research has identified high vehicle costs, limited charging infrastructure and policy conditions as important challenges to plug-in EV adoption (Dua et al., 2021). Similarly, Patel et al. (2024) highlighted limited charging infrastructure as an important barrier to EV adoption and highlighted the need for supportive government policies. These barriers and opportunities, however, may not be distributed equally across geographical areas. Overall, these studies show that the development of EVs is influenced by several factors and not simply by the availability of vehicles. However, these factors can vary across different geographical areas. Differences in economic activity, population, transportation needs, infrastructure, and market conditions may lead to different levels and rates of EV growth within the same state. The studies discussed above mainly focus on factors related to EV adoption, consumer attitudes, market conditions, barriers, and supporting infrastructure. These studies help explain the development of the EV market, but they do not directly show how registered EVs are distributed across different administrative areas within a state over several years. This is particularly important when analyzing EV registration data. A high number of registrations in a particular area shows that there is greater registration activity, but it does not necessarily mean that people in that area are more likely to adopt EVs individually. Therefore, registration numbers should be interpreted carefully when comparing areas with different population sizes and economic conditions. Absolute registration counts should therefore be interpreted as indicators of registration activity rather than as direct measures of individual-level EV adoption rates. 2.3 Vehicle-category differences and EV market development The transition toward electric mobility does not occur uniformly across vehicle categories. All electric vehicles differ in terms of purchase costs, travel patterns, energy requirements and charging behavior. In India, electric two-wheelers are mainly required for personal mobility, while electric three-wheelers play an important role in commercial transportation and Electric four-wheelers have different battery capacity, travel needs and charging requirements. Recent evidence indicates that India's electric mobility transition has been particularly strong in two- and three-wheelers (International Energy Agency, 2025). Overall EV registrations may hide important changes in the different vehicle categories. Analyzing vehicle categories is also important because it is important for infrastructure planning and various types of EVs may have different locations and charging needs. Analyzing EVs by vehicle category is important for understanding EV growth at the regional level. An RTO area with a high number of EV registrations may have a very different pattern of EV use from another area. For example, one area may have mostly electric two-wheelers, while another may have a higher share of three- or four-wheelers. These differences are important for understanding the nature of EV growth. Therefore, examining total EV registrations along with registrations by vehicle category provides a more complete understanding of regional EV development than looking at the total number of EVs alone. This category-based approach also adds to existing research by examining how different types of registered electric vehicles vary across RTO areas, rather than looking only at overall EV registrations or consumer preferences. 2.4 Geographical and temporal variation in EV adoption EV development can differ from one region to another because regions have different population, income levels, housing patterns, and infrastructure. These differences may be related to the way EV registrations are distributed across geographical areas. They are also important when considering the planning needs associated with electric mobility. Previous studies have highlighted the importance of geographical and transport-related information in planning charging infrastructure. He et al. (2015) showed that transportation networks can be considered when planning charging stations. Pagany et al. (2019) reviewed different approaches for identifying suitable locations for charging stations. He et al. (2022) noted that charging infrastructure planning needs to consider local conditions and demand rather than applying the same approach to all areas. Recent studies have also discussed the importance of accessibility, demand, and sustainable urban development (Zhang and Kontou, 2025; Wang et al., 2025). Although these studies highlight the importance of geographical information for EV planning, studies on charging locations and EV registrations examine different aspects. Charging-location studies mainly focus on where charging infrastructure should be placed, whereas registration data show where EVs are registered. Therefore, registration data can provide a useful starting point for understanding the distribution of EVs and changes in registration activity. This information can also be useful before conducting more detailed studies on EV demand or charging infrastructure requirements. However, registration data do not show how vehicles are used, where they travel, where they are charged, or the actual level of charging demand. The distinction between these two research areas is important for understanding the present study. Unlike studies that estimate charging infrastructure needs using travel patterns, demand, accessibility, or network information, the present study uses registration records to show where EVs are registered and how registration levels change over time. EV registrations also change over time. Changes in registration levels may be associated with developments in policies, vehicle availability, technology, and consumer acceptance. Examining registration data over several years can therefore show periods of higher or lower growth and changes in the types of EVs being registered. Different regions may also show different levels and rates of growth over the same period. Looking at geographical and yearly information together provides a clearer picture of these changes. For example, two RTO areas may have similar registration numbers in one year but may show different growth in later years. Analyzing several years of data can help identify areas that consistently record high registrations and areas where registration activity has increased more recently. Recent research has also highlighted the value of considering geographical and time-related information in transportation and infrastructure planning (Wang et al., 2025; Zhang and Kontou, 2025). In this study, geographical analysis refers mainly to the comparison of EV registrations across different RTO administrative areas. The study does not use formal spatial-statistical techniques. This distinction is important because RTO registration data show the administrative location where vehicles are registered, but they do not provide information about vehicle movement between areas, accessibility, travel distance, or the effect of neighboring regions. 2.5 EV registration data and research gap Vehicle-registration records are useful for studying the geographical and yearly development of EVs because they provide information on vehicles officially registered within specific administrative areas. Such data can help describe the level and distribution of EV registrations without requiring a primary survey. However, registration data have some limitations. They do not show how vehicles are used, how far they travel, where they are charged, how often they are charged, or why consumers choose EVs. They also cannot directly measure individual decisions to purchase or use an EV. Therefore, registration data are better treated as indicators of EV registration levels and geographical distribution rather than direct measures of consumer attitudes or individual adoption behavior. Another limitation is that areas with larger populations or larger vehicle markets may naturally have higher registration numbers. Therefore, a region with a high number of EV registrations does not necessarily have a higher EV adoption rate. Comparisons based only on total registrations should be understood as differences in registration levels unless population or vehicle-stock data are also considered. Previous research has examined factors related to EV adoption, consumer preferences, policy challenges, charging infrastructure, and EV market development. Studies such as Sierzchula et al. (2014), Rezvani et al. (2015), Coffman et al. (2017), and Degirmenci and Breitner (2017) have mainly examined factors related to EV adoption. Studies in India have also examined market conditions, barriers to EV adoption, charging infrastructure, and policy-related challenges (Kumar and Alok, 2020; Chhikara et al., 2021; Tarei et al., 2021; Mishra et al., 2021; Dua et al., 2021; Patel et al., 2024). These studies provide information on why consumers and markets may differ in their transition toward EVs. In contrast, geographical registration studies focus on where EVs are registered and how registration patterns change across different administrative areas. The present study focuses on this aspect using annual RTO-level registration data. These studies provide a useful basis for understanding how EV markets develop. However, the present study examines a different aspect: the geographical distribution and yearly changes in EV registrations across RTO administrative areas in Tamil Nadu. State-level registration totals may not show the differences between major cities, industrial regions, smaller urban areas, and other RTO regions. Therefore, more detailed geographical evidence is needed to understand how EV registrations are distributed within states and how this distribution changes over time. Such analysis can show where registrations are concentrated, whether the types of EVs registered differ between areas, and whether registration patterns become more widely distributed over time. Concentration measures can provide additional information for this type of analysis. For example, comparing the share of registrations held by the leading RTOs provides a simple way to examine geographical concentration. Measures such as the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) and Gini coefficient can also be used to examine how evenly EV registrations are distributed across administrative areas. However, a reduction in geographical concentration should not be considered evidence of higher individual EV adoption. It only shows that registrations are distributed across a wider range of areas. The present study addresses this research gap by examining RTO-level EV registrations across Tamil Nadu from 2022 to 2025. It examines annual registration trends, differences in registrations of electric two-wheelers, three-wheelers, and four-wheelers, differences between RTO areas, absolute and percentage changes, and the concentration of registrations among the leading RTOs. The study also compares Chennai with the wider EV registration pattern in Tamil Nadu. Chennai is a major metropolitan center, while the state also includes important industrial and urban areas such as Coimbatore, Tiruppur, Hosur, and Erode, as well as smaller RTO regions. Comparing these areas helps to understand whether EV registration growth is mainly concentrated in major urban centers or is becoming more widely distributed across the state. This comparison helps examine whether EV registration growth in Chennai followed a pattern similar to the overall growth in Tamil Nadu. The study is designed as a descriptive analysis of EV registration patterns at the RTO level rather than a causal study of EV adoption. It does not examine whether factors such as population, income, urbanization, policy incentives, economic conditions, or charging infrastructure explain regional differences because these variables are not included in the present dataset. Instead, the study provides a baseline description of geographical and yearly EV registration patterns that can support future research using demographic, socioeconomic, infrastructure, and policy-related variables. Overall, the study provides a detailed description of EV registration changes across Tamil Nadu between 2022 and 2025 and adds to the existing research on the geographical aspects of electric mobility. The analysis connects existing research on EV development with RTO-level registration data by showing how registration levels, vehicle categories, regional growth, and concentration vary across Tamil Nadu over time. The findings provide a useful base for future research on the demographic, economic, infrastructure, and policy factors that may be related to regional differences in EV development. 3 Materials and methods 3.1 Research design The study adopted a quantitative descriptive research design to examine the growth of Electric Vehicles (EVs) registrations across Tamil Nadu. The analyses mainly focused on changes in EVs registration over a time and different registration levels across Regional Transport Office (RTO) areas. The study used annual EV registration data at the RTO level to examine changes over time and differences across geographical areas. The geographical analysis was based on comparing registration levels across RTO areas rather than applying formal spatial techniques such as spatial autocorrelation or cluster analysis. Thus, the analysis focused on describing the variation and distribution of EV registrations across different RTO areas using aggregated secondary data. 3.2 Study area The study was conducted in Tamil Nadu, a major state in southern India with different types of urban and semi-urban areas. The state includes the Chennai metropolitan region, major cities, industrial centers and smaller towns. Tamil Nadu provides a suitable setting for studying differences in EV registrations because economic activity and transportation patterns vary across the state. Cities such as Chennai, Coimbatore, Tiruppur, Hosur, Erode and Vellore have different industrial, commercial and transportation characteristics. RTO areas were used to identify differences in EV registrations across different parts of Tamil Nadu. 3.3 Data source and study period The study used secondary data on EV registrations obtained from the Tamil Nadu government EV registration portal (https://tnev.tn.gov.in/ev-registration). The portal provides both current and historical information on electric vehicle registrations. The data can be viewed using filters based on vehicle type, RTO area and year. This allowed the study to obtain registration data for different vehicle categories and RTO areas over the selected period. The study used annual EV registration data for each RTO from 2022 to 2025. The data included three vehicle categories: electric two-wheelers, electric three-wheelers and electric four-wheelers. For each RTO and year, the total number of EV registrations was calculated by adding the registrations in these three categories. The data were then combined to obtain the annual total for Tamil Nadu and to compare EV registrations across different RTO areas. The RTO-level registration data used in the study are provided in the Supplementary Files. The study period was limited to 2022–2025 based on the historical data available from the Tamil Nadu Government TNEV EV Registration Portal on 26th August, 2026. The portal has since been updated with 2026 registration data, but these data were not included because the study period was already defined as 2022–2025. Since the study uses registration records, the results show patterns in recorded EV registrations and do not directly measure how individuals use their vehicles, travel or charge their EVs. The data show the number of EVs registered in each year. The figures were analyzed as yearly registrations and do not represent the total number of EVs currently in use. Therefore, the results describe the annual EV registration activity recorded in the government database. 3.4 Data preparation and standardization The data were organized in an Excel spreadsheet according to year, RTO area and vehicle category. Total EV registrations for each RTO and year were calculated by adding the registrations of electric two-wheelers, three-wheelers and four-wheelers. RTO/UO names were reviewed and standardized in Excel to maintain consistency across the four years of analysis. Minor differences in spelling, capitalization and naming formats were corrected so that the same RTO area was treated consistently across all years. The data were also checked for inconsistencies in RTO names and vehicle-category totals before the analysis. The portal data included 146 RTO/UO areas in 2022, 146 in 2023, 147 in 2024 and 147 in 2025. A year-wise comparison showed that the 2022 and 2023 lists contained the same 146 areas. Valparai UO was included as an additional area in 2024, increasing the total to 147 areas, and it remained in the 2025 data. The other RTO/UO areas were consistent across all four years. This difference in the number of areas was retained as reported in the source data. The annual RTO-level data were then compiled to obtain state-level and vehicle-category totals for each year. For the Chennai analysis, the relevant RTO areas were combined to examine the overall registration pattern within the Chennai metropolitan region. The analysis included TN01 Ayanavaram, TN02 Anna Nagar, TN03 Tondiarpet, TN04 Basin Bridge, TN05 Vyasarpadi, TN06 Mandaveli, TN07 Tiruvanmiyur, TN09 K.K. Nagar and TN10 Valasarawakkam. The prepared Excel dataset was used to calculate annual EV registrations, vehicle-category shares, year-on-year growth, absolute and percentage growth at the RTO level, registration concentration, and overall registration trends in Tamil Nadu. 3.5 Analytical measures 3.5.1 Annual EV registration Annual EV registrations were calculated by adding the total registrations recorded by all RTOs for each year. Annual EV Registrations = Sum of EV Registrations across all RTOs. This method was used to examine how EV registrations changed over time in Tamil Nadu. The annual total represents the registration activity recorded in the Tamil Nadu government EV registration database for each year and should be interpreted according to the definition provided by the source portal. 3.5.2 Vehicle-category share The contribution of each vehicle category to total EV registrations was calculated using: Vehicle-Category Share (%) = (EV Registrations of a Particular Vehicle Category / Total EV Registrations) × 100 This measure was used to identify the contribution of two-wheelers, three-wheelers, and four-wheelers to total EV registrations. 3.5.3 RTO growth rate RTO Growth Rate was used to identify the change in EV registrations from 1 year to the next. RTO Growth Rate (%) = [2025 RTO Registrations – 2022 RTO Registrations / 2022 RTO Registrations] × 100 This measure shows the annual increase or decrease in RTO growth registrations. 3.5.4 RTO-level growth rate The change in EV registrations for each RTO between 2022 and 2025 was calculated using: RTO-level Growth Rate (%) = [Current Year RTO Registrations—Previous Year RTO Registrations / Previous Year RTO Registrations] × 100 In addition, the actual change in registrations were calculated as Absolute change = EV Registrations in 2025—EV Registrations in 2022 Both measures were used because percentage growth may appear high when the initial number of registrations is small. Absolute change shows the actual number of EV registrations that increased or decreased. 3.6 RTO-level geographical analysis RTO-level geographical analysis was conducted by comparing total EV registrations across RTO areas for each year of the study period. The analysis focused on identifying: RTOs with high EV registrations; changes in the leading RTOs over time; and differences in EV registrations across areas. The ten RTOs with the highest total EV registrations in 2025 were also identified to show the main registration centers in the final year of the study. In this study, the term “geographical analysis” refers to differences in EV registrations across RTO administrative areas. The analysis is descriptive and does not apply formal spatial-statistical techniques such as spatial autocorrelation, spatial clustering, or geographically weighted analysis. Therefore, the geographical findings are interpreted as differences in registration activity between administrative areas rather than as evidence of spatial dependence or geographical causation. 3.7 Registration concentration analysis Registration concentration was examined by calculating the proportion of total EV registrations represented by the five and ten highest-registration RTOs. The Top-5 concentration measure was calculated as: Top-5 Concentration Share (%) = (Total EV Registrations of the Five Highest-Registration RTOs / Total EV Registrations in Tamil Nadu) × 100 The Top-10 concentration measure was calculated as: Top-10 Concentration Share (%) = (Total EV Registrations of the Ten Highest-Registration RTOs / Total EV Registrations in Tamil Nadu) × 100 A decline in these measures shows that EV registrations are spreading to more areas instead of being concentrated in a few leading RTOs. 3.8 Chennai urban EV regions A separate analysis was conducted for Chennai because the metropolitan area contains multiple RTO areas. The selected Chennai RTOs areas were combined to understand the overall EV trend in the metropolitan region. 3.8.1 The Chennai analysis included the following RTO areas: TN01—Ayanavaram, TN02—Anna Nagar, TN03—Tondiarpet, TN04—Basin Bridge, TN05—Vyasarpadi, TN06—Mandaveli, TN07—Tiruvanmiyur, TN09—K.K. Nagar, and TN10—Valasarawakkam. The combined Chennai total was calculated by summarizing the EV registrations recorded for these RTO areas for each year. The analysis examined changes in Total EV registrations; Electric two-wheeler registrations; Electric three-wheeler registrations; and Electric four-wheeler registrations. 3.9 Analytical procedure The analysis was conducted in the following sequence: Collection and compilation of annual RTO-level EV registration data. Review and standardization of RTO names and codes across the study years. Calculation of total EV registrations for each RTO and year by adding the three vehicle categories. Calculation of state-wide registrations for each vehicle category. Calculation of annual vehicle-category shares. Calculation of year-on-year growth in total EV registrations. Comparison of EV registrations across RTO areas. Calculation of absolute and percentage growth between 2022 and 2025. Calculation of Top-5 and Top-10 geographical concentration of EV registrations. Calculation of EV registrations across the selected Chennai RTO areas. Calculation of Chennai's share of total Tamil Nadu EV registrations for each year. Identification of the leading RTOs and the Top-−5 and Top-−10 RTO composition for each year. The analysis was primarily descriptive and comparative. The registration data were organized and analyzed using a spreadsheet. The findings were interpreted to understand changes in EV registrations over time and across different areas of Tamil Nadu. 3.10 Methodological rationale The use of annual RTO-level registration data was suitable for the objectives of this study for several reasons. First, annual data help to identify changes in EV registrations over time without focusing on short-term monthly changes. Second, RTO-level data provide more detailed information about geographical differences than state-level data. State-level totals can show whether EV registrations are increasing, but they cannot show which areas are experiencing this growth. Third, the study used total registrations, vehicle-category shares, absolute growth and percentage growth to understand EV growth from different perspectives. Percentage growth shows the relative increase, while absolute growth shows the actual increase in the number of registrations. Fourth, the Top-5 and Top-10 concentration measures help to show whether EV registrations are concentrated in a few RTO areas or becoming more widely distributed across the state. Fifth, the separate Chennai analysis allows the registration trend of the major metropolitan region to be compared with the overall Tamil Nadu trend. Examining Chennai's share of total state registrations provides an additional descriptive measure for understanding whether registration activity is becoming more geographically distributed beyond the selected Chennai RTO areas. Finally, the overall EV registration tends across Chennai were examined. Overall, these measures provide a structured approach to understanding changes in EV registrations over time, differences between vehicle categories and geographical variation across Tamil Nadu. 4 Results 4.1 Temporal evolution of electric vehicle registration The analysis of RTO-level registration data shows an overall increase in electric vehicles registrations in Tamil Nadu between 2022 and 2025. EV registrations increased substantially from 69,785 in 2022 to 1,65,818 in 2025. The highest annual growth was recorded in 2024 at 45.97%. However, the growth rate decreased to 25.89% in 2025, the total number of EV registrations continued to increase. This shows that EV registrations continued to increase during the study period, although the year-on-year growth rate was lower in 2025 than in 2024. This finding is in line with recent research showing continued growth in the Indian electric vehicle market. However, the present study adds further detail by examining changes in EV registrations across different RTO areas in Tamil Nadu, rather than looking only at national or state-level data. The registration data, however, do not provide information on the reasons for this growth. The vehicle-category results show that electric two-wheelers had the highest number of registrations throughout the study period. This finding is consistent with the wider pattern of electric mobility in India, where two-wheelers form an important part of the EV market. Recent studies in India have also examined electric two-wheelers because of their importance in personal transportation, considering factors such as price, charging time, infrastructure availability and vehicle features. However, the share of two-wheelers decreased over time, while registrations of three-wheelers and four-wheelers increased. The increase in four-wheeler registrations shows a change in the composition of registered EVs during the study period. Unlike studies that mainly examine consumers' intention to purchase electric two-wheelers, the present study describes actual registration records across three vehicle categories. Therefore, the study adds a category-based view of EV registrations rather than examining the factors behind individual purchase decisions. The RTO-level results show differences in EV registrations across Tamil Nadu. Tiruppur North, Coimbatore Central, Tiruppur South and Coimbatore North were among the RTOs with high registration levels in 2025. Erode and Hosur also recorded high levels of EV registrations. These results show that considerable registration activity occurred outside Chennai and that several other urban and regional centers contributed to the total EV registrations in the state. This finding is consistent with previous research showing geographical differences in EV-related demand and market development in India. However, the present study examines registered vehicles at the RTO level, whereas many earlier studies have focused on consumer preferences, purchase intentions or EV demand in selected cities. Thus, the present analysis provides a different type of geographical evidence based on registration records. The comparison of RTO-level registrations shows the importance of considering both actual increases and percentage growth. Avinashi UO recorded the highest percentage growth among the RTOs presented. However, this high percentage was partly due to its low number of registrations in 2022. In comparison, Tiruppur North recorded the largest actual increase in EV registrations. Therefore, percentage growth alone may not provide a complete picture of EV growth across different RTOs. Considering both the actual increase and percentage growth provides a clearer view of changes in EV registrations. This is particularly important because RTO areas may have very different starting levels. A small initial number of registrations can result in a high percentage increase even when the actual increase is relatively small. The registration concentration results show a lower share of registrations among the leading RTO areas over the study period. The share of total EV registrations from the top five RTOs decreased from 14.24% in 2022 to 11.28% in 2025. Similarly, the share of the top ten RTOs decreased from 24.52% to 19.90%. Although there was a small increase in concentration between 2024 and 2025, the overall trend from 2022 to 2025 shows a decrease in the share held by the leading RTOs. This means that the top five and top ten RTOs accounted for a smaller proportion of total registrations in 2025 than in 2022. However, these measures only show the share held by the leading RTO groups and do not provide a complete measure of how evenly registrations were distributed across all RTO areas. Therefore, the findings should be understood as changes in the share of the leading RTOs rather than as evidence of an equal distribution of EV registrations across Tamil Nadu. The Chennai results show that EV registrations increased steadily between 2022 and 2025, with total registrations rising from 11,663 to 17,499. Two-wheelers remained the largest category, but their share decreased from 86.41% in 2022 to 66.36% in 2025. In contrast, three-wheelers and four-wheelers increased, with four-wheelers showing the highest growth. The Chennai results also provide a useful comparison with the wider Tamil Nadu pattern. Chennai registrations increased by approximately 50.03% between 2022 and 2025, while total Tamil Nadu registrations increased by 137.61% during the same period. Chennai therefore recorded considerable growth, but the increase was lower than the growth observed across Tamil Nadu as a whole. Chennai's share of total Tamil Nadu EV registrations also changed during the study period. This provides further evidence that the increase in EV registrations across the state was not limited to Chennai. The comparison therefore helps place Chennai's registration growth within the wider state-level pattern. The findings on vehicle categories and geographical differences can also be compared with earlier research on EV development in India. Previous studies have mainly examined consumer acceptance, purchase intentions, vehicle features, policy measures and charging infrastructure. For example, studies on electric two-wheelers have examined factors such as price, charging time, infrastructure and vehicle features in relation to purchase or acceptance decisions. The present study does not examine these factors. Instead, it provides descriptive information on the registration patterns observed across different RTO areas and vehicle categories. This distinction is important because registration data alone cannot explain why one RTO area recorded more EV registrations than another. Research on charging infrastructure has also used geographical, demand and spatial-planning information to identify suitable locations for charging facilities. Such studies show the importance of geographical information for infrastructure planning, but they require information beyond registration counts. The present study provides a descriptive starting point by showing where EVs are registered and how registration levels change over time. However, the data do not provide information on vehicle use, travel patterns, charging behavior or actual charging demand. Therefore, the findings should not be used to directly determine the number, location or capacity of charging facilities required. Similarly, the present study identifies differences in registration levels and growth across RTO areas but does not explain the reasons for these differences. Factors such as population, income, urbanization, economic activity, vehicle ownership, policy measures and charging infrastructure may be related to regional differences, but these factors were not examined in the present study. This differs from studies that directly examine EV adoption or demand using socioeconomic, behavioral, vehicle or infrastructure-related factors. Future research incorporating such information could provide a better understanding of the differences observed across RTO areas. Overall, the findings show three main characteristics of EV registrations in Tamil Nadu during 2022–2025: an increase in total registrations, changes in the composition of registrations across vehicle categories, and a lower share of registrations accounted for by the leading RTOs. While earlier studies have mainly examined consumer factors, vehicle features, policy measures or charging infrastructure, the present study provides a descriptive RTO-level view of actual EV registration records over multiple years. The study therefore adds sub-state geographical evidence to the existing literature and provides a useful base for future research combining registration data with demographic, socioeconomic, infrastructure and policy information. 6 Limitations and future direction This study has some limitations which provide opportunities for future research. The study uses EV registration data, which indicate registration activity but do not provide information on vehicle usage, travel behavior or charging behavior. Future studies can combine registration records with survey or other primary data to examine these aspects. RTO areas are administrative units and may not fully represent the travel patterns or actual movement of EV users. Future research can incorporate more detailed geographical information, such as geographic coordinates, road networks or other spatial units, to examine the geographical distribution of EVs in greater detail. The study covers the period from 2022 to 2025. Future studies can use longer time periods to examine changes in EV registrations over a longer period and identify whether the observed trends continue. The present analysis does not include socioeconomic, demographic or charging-infrastructure variables. These variables may help explain differences in EV registrations across RTO areas. Future research can integrate such variables to examine the factors associated with regional variation. The study uses absolute registration counts at the RTO level. These counts may be affected by differences in population size, vehicle ownership and the overall number of vehicles across areas. Future studies can examine registration rates using appropriate population or vehicle-base denominators where reliable data are available. The study focuses on Tamil Nadu. Similar analyses can be conducted for other Indian states to compare regional registration patterns and examine differences in EV growth across geographical contexts. 7 Conclusion The study analyzed EV registrations across RTO areas in Tamil Nadu from 2022 to 2025. The findings show that there is an increase in EV registrations. However, EV growth varied across RTOs and Vehicle categories. The RTO-level analysis shows that EV registrations differed across Tamil Nadu. Some major urban areas recorded high numbers of registrations, while other RTOs showed significant growth. The findings also show that both the actual increase and percentage growth are important when comparing different RTOs. A high percentage increase may occur when the initial number of registrations is small. The study therefore provides a descriptive picture of the temporal, vehicle-category and RTO-level patterns of EV registrations in Tamil Nadu. The findings can provide useful background information for transportation and electric mobility planning. However, registration data should not be treated as direct measures of EV usage, charging demand or individual-level adoption. Future research can combine RTO registration data with demographic, socioeconomic, population, vehicle-base and charging-infrastructure data to examine the factors associated with regional differences in EV registrations. More detailed spatial data and longer time periods can also provide a stronger basis for understanding the changing geographical pattern of electric vehicle registrations in Tamil Nadu and other Indian states.
