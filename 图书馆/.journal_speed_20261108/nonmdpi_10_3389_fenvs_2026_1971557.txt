You can register here Notify me Abstract Allowance banking shifts emissions across periods, while a bank-dependent reserve can reduce current issuance. This study evaluates those mechanisms in a stylized aggregate simulation using 900 common eight-period paths. The main comparison holds reduction strength and rebound response fixed and changes only the reserve rule. A factorial experiment separates this accounting change from the unequal response coefficients in the original policy packages. With matched coefficients, the reserve reduces mean annual emissions from 67.028 to 66.882 index units, a difference of −0.145 with a paired Monte Carlo interval of [−0.155, −0.136]. Annual target-exceedance frequency falls from 0.3015 to 0.2688, a reduction of 3.278 percentage points. The illustrative cost proxy rises from 17.332 to 19.321, while within-path price-index standard deviation falls from 4.307 to 3.905. The terminal private bank decreases from 17.776 to 10.155; most reduced issuance therefore appears as a smaller remaining bank rather than immediate emissions reduction. Factorial decomposition shows that the much larger original package contrast combines reduction strength, rebound attenuation, and reserve activation. Sensitivity tests show negligible or zero annual target benefit at some settings. The model has no endogenous market price, firm optimization, or welfare estimate. Its results describe conditional accounting trade-offs, not effects or optimal rules in an actual emissions-trading system.
