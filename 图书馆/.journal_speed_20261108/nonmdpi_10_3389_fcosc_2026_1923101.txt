You can register here Notify me Abstract Introduction: The climate crisis presents an immediate threat to human health. Despite this reality, psychological distress in the face of the climate breakdown is at risk of being treated as a 'mental health condition'. The Power Threat Meaning Framework (PTMF) offers a new perspective that centers power and contextualizes distress through the development of narratives. This paper reports on the first peer-reviewed study of the fit between the PTMF and climate-related distress. The focus was on experiences of climate activists who are highly engaged with the climate crisis. Methods: Serial semi-structured interviews were held with eight UK-based climate activists to co-construct PTMF narratives. Thirty interviews were analyzed with template analysis. A priori themes from the PTMF were used to inform the coding template. Results: Participants' experiences could readily be mapped onto the five interrelated domains of the PTMF: Power, Threats, Meanings, Threat Responses and Strengths. A meta-narrative was produced through synthesizing participants' accounts. Discussion: People's distress and experiences of environmental action vary based on operations of power and systemic injustices, both within and outside of activist spaces. Findings support calls to understand individual and collective responses to climate breakdown through the lens of collective trauma. The PTMF may offer a useful tool to understand climate-related distress and encourage wider public engagement with the climate crisis.
