You can register here Notify me Abstract China's transition toward a green economy remains constrained by rising energy consumption and persistently high carbon emissions. As a transformative force reshaping production processes and resource allocation, the digital economy (DE) is increasingly regarded as an important contributor to green development. Using panel data for 284 Chinese cities from 2011 to 2021, this study applies a spatial Durbin model (SDM) and a geographically and temporally weighted regression (GTWR) model to examine the direct and spatial spillover effects of DE and its key dimensions on urban green development, as well as the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of these relationships. The results reveal that: (1) the estimated direct and spatial spillover effects of DE on green development are significantly positive, with significantly positive estimated effects for digital industrialization and digital application, whereas the effects of digital infrastructure are not statistically significant; (2) the estimated local coefficients of DE on green development exhibit an overall descriptive "rise-then-decline" temporal pattern, with digital industrialization and digital infrastructure showing broadly similar trajectories; (3) substantial spatial heterogeneity is observed in the DE-green development relationship, with relatively stronger positive local coefficients concentrated in many central and western cities, whereas weaker or negative coefficients are more frequently observed in southeastern coastal areas, a pattern also observed for digital infrastructure and digital application. By identifying spatial spillovers, spatiotemporal heterogeneity, and dimension-specific patterns in the DE-green development relationship, this study advances understanding of digitalization-driven green transitions and highlights the need for regionally differentiated digital development and environmental policies.
