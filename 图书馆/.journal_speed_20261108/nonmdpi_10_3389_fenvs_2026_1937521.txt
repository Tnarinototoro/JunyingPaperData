You can register here Notify me Abstract Sustainable development, as a widely accepted principle, has gradually been embedded in the realm of international investment in recent decades. The three intersecting elements of sustainable development—economic development, social well-being, and environmental protection—have a close nexus with international investment. This paper aims to examine the sustainable development requirements in China’s current international investment agreements (IIAs), including both bilateral investment treaties (BITs) and free trade agreements (FTAs), and to explore the approaches to integrate sustainable development into international investment. This paper contributes to the existing literature with a systematic treaty-content analysis of China’s IIAs, supported by descriptive statistics. The findings show that among the 124 IIAs that China has concluded as of 20 September 2026, only 29 of them have pursued sustainable development. The identified sustainable development requirements include three categories: preambular paragraphs, exceptional clauses, and calls for cooperation. Compared to global efforts, the sustainable development provisions in China’s IIAs cannot provide strong support for the regulation of international investment due to their limited amounts, general and abstract stipulations, and imbalance of rights and duties between investors and host countries. Therefore, this research suggests that China’s IIAs should incorporate more sustainable development provisions, reaffirm the regulatory power of host countries, impose sustainability-related responsibilities on investors, and adjust dispute resolution provisions for sustainable development measures.
