Abstract
Urban green spaces (UGS) are essential to climate-responsive urban planning, yet evaluating ecological functionality beyond spatial provision remains challenging in urbanizing warm-humid cities. This study develops an integrated socio-ecological framework for neighbourhood-scale UGS functionality in Rajarhat-Newtown, Kolkata, India, combining GIS-based vegetation assessment, systematic field validation, supporting microclimatic observations, and resident perception. Vegetation performance was assessed using a locally calibrated vegetation proxy cut-off (Soil Adjusted Vegetation Index, SAVI ≥ 0.30), derived from Landsat 8 imagery through iterative field-reference assessment. A questionnaire survey assessed usage, accessibility, satisfaction, and design expectations. Area-based analysis showed that 78.5% of the mapped area within the 25 residential blocks had SAVI values below the locally calibrated 0.30 cut-off, indicating predominantly sparse or low-performing vegetation conditions. Supporting observations at two residential settings recorded lower mean air temperature and higher relative humidity near a densely vegetated pocket forest than near a lawn-dominated UGS, providing limited local evidence consistent with the vegetation assessment. The chi-square test identified no statistically significant categorical association between visitation frequency and user satisfaction, whereas Spearman’s rank correlation identified a weak but statistically significant positive monotonic relationship (ρ = 0.216, p = 0.025, n = 107). Frequent use alone therefore did not adequately explain perceived UGS quality; residents preferred canopy cover, shading, and distributed vegetation networks. Integrating SAVI-derived and field-informed vegetation-condition evidence with resident perception differentiates comparatively higher- and low-performing vegetation proxy conditions and supports performance-oriented rather than allocation-based green-space planning. The transferable framework supports climate-responsive neighbourhood planning and ecological engineering in warm-humid cities.
