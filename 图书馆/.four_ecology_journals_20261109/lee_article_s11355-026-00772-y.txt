Abstract
While practicing qigong in natural settings reportedly enhances its benefits, the influence of specific landscape attributes has not been studied extensively. According to the Landscape Preference Matrix, coherence and complexity are key factors in determining environmental preference. Unlike research on meditation, which considers complexity (such as plant diversity), studies on qigong tend to focus more on coherence, leaving the role of complexity and its potential impact on practice efficacy in urban settings unexplored. Because urban qigong takes place in designed greenspace, the answer bears directly on how planting in small urban sites is specified. To address this gap, this study asked 25 experienced participants to practice qigong in three different environments in a within-subjects design: one with complex vegetation, one with coherent vegetation, and a non-vegetated control environment. The physiological indices did not differ significantly between environments, although an exploratory pairwise comparison showed a lower heart rate in the complex-planting condition than in the control. Both vegetated environments yielded significantly higher qi intensity, qi experience, and environmental preference than the control, with no difference between the two planting configurations. For practice, the findings tentatively suggest that a species-rich planting can be introduced in a small urban space without loss of perceived coherence, so that biodiversity-oriented planting and health-supportive design need not be traded off against each other.
