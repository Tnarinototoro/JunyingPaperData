Abstract
As urban ecological conservation receives increasing attention, coordinating green infrastructure (GI) spatial structure with biodiversity conservation has become an important issue in urban ecological planning. However, a highly structured GI does not necessarily correspond to high bird habitat (BH) suitability, and existing research has paid relatively little attention to the spatial congruence and mismatch relationships between the two, and how these can be further translated into ecological network planning strategies. To address this issue, this study employs Hangzhou as a case study. Green infrastructure assessment (GIA) was applied to evaluate GI spatial structure across three dimensions: scale, morphology, and pattern. The Maximum Entropy (MAXENT) model is utilized to assess BH suitability. The four-quadrant model is applied to identify spatial congruence and mismatch zones between GI and BH, based on which three optimization objectives are proposed. Using source patch extraction- resistance surface construction-circuit theory, multi-objective ecological networks (ENs) are developed. Finally, structural resilience assessments of connectivity and robustness are conducted for three ENs using connectivity indices and network attack simulations. The principal findings of this study include: (1) The spatial structure of GI and BH suitability generally exhibit a positive spatial correlation; with some regions displaying high or low values align, while other regions demonstrate mismatched relationships. (2) The three ENs exhibit distinct differences in source area size and composition, corridor number and length, and network topology, reflecting the spatial organizational characteristics associated with different objectives. (3) EN structures that achieve dual-objective coordination exhibit optimal resilience. This study clarified the spatial relationship between GI structure and BH suitability and provided methodological insights for integrating biodiversity conservation with the allocation of blue-green spaces to support sustainable urban development.
