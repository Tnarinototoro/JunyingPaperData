Abstract
Climate change is one of the major drivers of changes in the natural habitats of many insect pests worldwide. The white-striped longhorn beetle Batocera lineolata Chevrolat, 1852 (Coleoptera: Cerambycidae) is a serious pest species currently causing severe damage to Eucalyptus L’Hér., 1789 (Myrtales: Myrtaceae) plantation forests in Northern Vietnam. Understanding the geographical distribution characteristics of this pest is important for forest management strategies, especially under a changing climate. Therefore, this study sought to clarify environmental factors associated with the presence of B. lineolata and to predict its current and future habitat suitability areas. Distribution data of the pest were collected from field surveys in seven provinces in Northern Vietnam, and the species distribution model was developed using Maxent and 11 bioclimatic and topographical variables. Our study showed that the selected model had a good performance (test AUC = 0.878) compared with the null model (test AUC = 0.539). Mean diurnal temperature range (Bio 2, 21.5%), temperature annual range (Bio 7, 16.1%), and temperature seasonality (Bio 4, 13.7%) were the three most important variables in predicting the potential distribution of B. lineolata. The very high suitability area in the current distribution of B. lineolata was 4,876 km2, occupying about 4% of the study area. The very high suitability area of this pest is projected to expand in the 2070s under the shared socio-economic pathway (SSP245) scenario, and in the 2050s and 2070s under the SSP585 scenario. These findings would provide valuable insights for forest management strategies concerning B. lineolata in Vietnam.
