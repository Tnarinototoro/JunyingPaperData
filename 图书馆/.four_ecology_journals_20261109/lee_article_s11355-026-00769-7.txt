Abstract
In China, driven by policies that promote the concentrated and contiguous preservation of traditional villages, the concept of regional and holistic conservation has gained broad acceptance among academia and practitioners. As heritage-preserving entities, traditional villages can be conceptualized as populations sharing and embodying traditional Chinese culture. This study adopts a population-ecology perspective, analyzing 413 traditional villages in Jiangxi Province, to examine village clustering patterns and population relationships, identify traditional village clusters, and propose cluster-based conservation measures. Ripley’s K function, kernel density analysis, entropy analysis, gravity models, and hierarchical nearest-neighbor clustering were employed to examine village clustering patterns and population relationships. The most pronounced clustering effect in Jiangxi’s traditional villages occurs within a 30 km radius. Villages with strong and those with weak competitiveness are spatially intermixed. Villages exhibiting strong symbiosis are concentrated in the Luling Cultural Zone, Linchuan Cultural Zone, and Huizhou Cultural Zone. Jiangxi Province contains nine traditional-village clusters, including the Fuliang Cluster and Wuyuan Cluster, primarily distributed in central and eastern Jiangxi Province. Each cluster region should leverage traditional culture to rebuild competitive advantages, enhance symbiosis through key nodes, and strengthen the circulation of key factors to enhance agglomeration effects. Following the ‘population characteristics-population relationships-population evolution’ framework, the delineation of traditional village clusters based on population traits and relationships, and the proposal of conservation strategies, offer a potentially effective pathway for protecting traditional village clusters.
