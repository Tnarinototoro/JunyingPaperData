Abstract
This study aimed to investigate the difference in bird collisions related to stickers on the glass of noise barriers in Ansung and Pyeongtaek cities, Gyeonggi province, South Korea. We surveyed for bird collisions at each noise barrier in every three weeks from September 2023 to August 2024. We measured landscape variables (e.g., coverage of paved areas, buildings, agricultural land, bare ground, woody vegetation, and surface of water bodies) within a 500 m radius from the noise barriers using QGIS. The coverage of all landscape variables did not differ between noise barriers with and without stickers. The number of carcasses and species was higher in noise barriers without stickers than in those with stickers. The size of the glass, land cover, shape, and season were highly related to the number of carcasses and species of collided birds. The results of our study suggest that constructing noise barriers with stickers on the glass may be an effective method to reduce bird collisions. Further research is required to investigate the conservation implications of collisions concerning bird community and their habitats.
