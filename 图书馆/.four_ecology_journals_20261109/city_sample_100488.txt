ABSTRACT
Climate change and environmental degradation increasingly threaten urban public health, yet mon-
itoring infrastructure is often unavailable in resource-constrained settings. This study develops and
critically evaluates a machine learning framework that predicts environmental health-relatedsearch
behavior, used as a low-cost digital proxy rather than a measure of clinical burden, in Morocco
using exclusively open-access global datasets. We integrated five years of weekly data (April 2020
to April 2025) from ERA5 meteorological reanalysis, Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service
products, and Google Trends to model binary high-interest states for four search categories: air
quality,respiratoryconditions,heat-relatedillness,andallergies.Ensemblemodelswith71engineered
features achieved strong classification performance (ROC-AUC 0.92 to 0.96) with temporal stability
over 52-week horizons. An ablation study showed that this discrimination is carried primarily by the
autocorrelated search signal; the environmental reanalysis block added negligible incremental ROC-
AUC at national scale, yet retained meaningful standalone signal for the respiratory and air-quality
categories while collapsing to near-random for allergy and heat-related illness. This contrast tracks
biological plausibility, which varied substantially across categories: respiratory predictions aligned
withestablishedepidemiologicalpatterns,whereasheat-relatedpredictionsshowedweaktemperature
correspondence and implausible winter peaks. We therefore frame the system as a complementary
screening layer pending external validation against clinical endpoints, not as a validated disease-
surveillance or operational warning system. Because it relies solely on freely available data, the
framework offers a replicable national-scale prototype for environmental health monitoring across
North Africa and the Global South, contributing to SDG 3, 11, and 13.

This study develops a machine learning framework for119
predicting environmental health search patterns in Morocco120
usingfreelyavailableglobaldatasets.Followingestablished121
best practices for environmental health prediction [14, 28],122
the framework consists of: (1) data acquisition from ERA5123
