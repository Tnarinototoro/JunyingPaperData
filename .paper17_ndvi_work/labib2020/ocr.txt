Metrics representing exposure tO the natural environment are widely used in environmental health-related
studies. They are calculated using a variety Of different data sources representlng greenspace and a range 0f
buffer sizes representing human interaction with the envlronment. Previous studies have identified issues re-
lating tO buffer distance and scaling effects on greenspace exposure assessments when using satellite image-
derived metrics. We evaluate the spatial scale sensitivity of three common greenspace metrics (i.e., Normalised
Difference Vegetation lndex- NDVI ， Leaf Area lndex- LAI, and Land Use and Land Cover-LULC), using lacunarity
analysis ， as a scale-dependent measure Of heterogeneity based on the principles Of fractals. By producing a
'lacunarity curve' across multiple spatial scales, we defined the scale-vanances for specific greenspace metrics,
including the upper scale limit at which the metrics become invariant ， approximately 640 m for Sentinel-2 and
480 m for Landsat-8. Each Of the greenspace metrics we considered exhibited scale sensitivities ， meaning that
each is expected tO have a different influence on the strength and significance Of the statistical associations found

lacunarity curves, we produced a novel composite ， multi-scale greenspace exposure index' in which each input
scale is weighted according tO its relative scale sensitivity. We also created a multi-scale, multi-metric map

gradients across a large urban conurbation are even more marked when using our multl-scale 'exposure index ，
maps compared tO traditional approaches. Our multi-scale ， composite greenspace exposure index ， mapping
techniques are not as vulnerable tO scale effects as traditional approaches and can be readily transferred tO the
